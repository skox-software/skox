Prepared September 9, 2026 for SKOX. Internal working brief; not a client attachment. Public-source research plus Josh’s visit today. No merchant account, order statements, private analytics or customer data were accessed. No contact has been made.

## Recommendation

Pursue this as a focused website and direct-pickup project. The public customer journey has a credible gap: the café’s Linktree sends people to marketplaces, while a clearly discoverable owned website and direct-ordering route were not found in the paths reviewed. That is an opportunity to investigate, not evidence of a known revenue loss.

Lead with **“Make it easy for your regulars to find you and order ahead.”** Offer to check what their existing ordering subscription can already do. A fast, well-designed site can express their coffee identity, put practical information in one place and make pickup obvious. Avoid selling a bespoke ordering app before checking native options.

This is a promising local lead, but budget, decision-maker, order volume and willingness to change remain unknown. A lack of a visible website does not establish buying intent or a large project budget.

## Their strengths and what to build around

The café identifies with Colombian coffee. Its marketplace menu includes Campesino, café con leche, filtered brewing methods, arepas, empanadas and breakfast items. That supports a more distinctive presentation than a generic coffee template. Menu availability and sourcing claims need owner approval before publication. [Brand Linktree](https://linktr.ee/sanjulianscoffeebar), [Uber Eats menu](https://www.ubereats.com/store/san-julians-coffee-bar/DWgSDzGPX5yh5H5sigTAWw).

Recommended positioning hypothesis: **Colombian coffee and a neighborhood breakfast stop, with an easy way to order ahead.** Emphasize real food and drinks, the actual space and the people behind it. Ask for the current coffee sourcing story instead of copying historical claims about a particular farm.

Likely customer jobs to validate with staff:

- A repeat morning customer wants their usual drink and breakfast with a clear pickup time.
- A first-time local visitor wants the menu, opening hours, location and a reason to choose this café.
- Someone sent by Instagram wants to act immediately after seeing a drink or dish.
- A nearby office may want larger breakfast orders, but catering demand and capacity are unverified; keep that outside the first build.

These are product hypotheses, not survey findings. A nearby precedent exists: [Foxtail’s Winter Park location page](https://www.foxtailcoffee.com/locations/foxtail-coffee-winter-park) puts store information and a mobile-order action together. It demonstrates a local convenience standard, not proof of San Julián’s demand or competitors’ conversion rates.

## Ordering options and honest economics

[Toast Online Ordering](https://pos.toasttab.com/products/online-ordering), updated September 3, 2026, advertises direct ordering with POS/kitchen integration. The first implementation choice should be a branded website linking to the café’s verified provider-hosted checkout. Embedded/custom-domain options depend on the package. Confirm subscription, payment processing, menus, modifiers, preparation times, taxes and order routing with the owner/provider. Native functionality may solve most of the operational need.

DoorDash’s advertised marketplace plans currently list **15%, 25% or 30% delivery commission and 6% pickup commission**, subject to terms; this is not their contract. [DoorDash marketplace overview](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/marketplace/how-it-works-for-restaurants).

DoorDash also offers direct Online Ordering: its fee explanation lists zero commission with processing of **3.3% + $0.30 on Starter, or 2.9% + $0.30 on Boost/Pro**, with package costs to verify. Marketplace processing is included in its commission. Therefore compare existing DoorDash direct options with Toast’s actual quote; “DoorDash always takes 30%” would be misleading. [DoorDash fee explanation](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/learning-center/delivery-commission).

### Illustrative pickup comparison, not a forecast or Toast quote

Assume a $20 pre-tax basket, a 6% marketplace pickup commission and hypothetical direct processing of 2.9% + $0.30. Hold selling price and fulfillment constant; omit tax/tip processing effects, promotions and other adjustments for this simplified example.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketplace pickup fee | $20 × 6% | $1.20/order |
| Direct processing | $20 × 2.9% + $0.30 | $0.88/order |
| Potential difference | $1.20 - $0.88 | $0.32/order |
| At 300 genuinely shifted pickup orders/month | 300 × $0.32 | $96/month before recurring costs |
| At 500 shifted orders/month | 500 × $0.32 | $160/month before recurring costs |
| $2,500 build recovery from fee difference alone | $2,500 ÷ $0.32 | About 7,813 shifted orders, before recurring costs |

At a $15 basket the same hypothetical difference is only $0.165/order. At 3.3% + $0.30 processing, the $20-basket difference falls to $0.24/order. These numbers show why the sales case must include customer convenience and additional profitable business, not just commission removal.

Delivery requires its own comparison. On a hypothetical $20 order at 25% marketplace commission, the merchant fee is $5. If direct processing costs $0.88 and an assumed courier charge is $6 paid by the café, direct costs total $6.88, which is $1.88 more. The $6 is an illustrative assumption, not a quoted rate. If the customer pays delivery, measure the effect on their total price and willingness to order. [Toast’s Uber Direct partner description](https://pos.toasttab.com/partners/directory/uber-direct) confirms that delivery fees depend on factors such as distance and region; a commission-free channel is not cost-free.

Use actual contribution economics:

**Monthly incremental contribution = shifted comparable orders × verified net fee difference + truly additional orders × contribution per additional order - new fixed costs - extra operating costs.**

Separate orders shifted from counter payment, marketplace pickup and marketplace delivery. Moving an existing counter sale online can add fees without adding revenue. Additional order contribution must account for ingredients, packaging, payment fees and incremental labor. A before/after increase alone does not prove the website caused it.

## Proposed SKOX offer

**Recommended draft price: $2,500 one-time.** This is a suggested SKOX quote for discussion, not an established company rate or accepted commitment. Proposed payment schedule: 50% at kickoff, 50% after the agreed acceptance checks and before launch. Target seven business days after content, account access and provider readiness are confirmed. Scope changes or provider delays revise the date in writing.

Include one custom mobile-friendly landing page; up to six featured menu items with a link to the provider-managed full menu; coffee/story and visit sections; a prominent Order Pickup action; integration with an existing eligible provider-hosted checkout; preparation of website/order links for Google Business Profile and social profiles; one QR counter-card design; basic page metadata and local business structured data using confirmed details; click measurement; two consolidated revision rounds; staff handoff; 30 days of correction support for delivered scope; and one 30-day results review.

Direct menu/payment management stays in the ordering provider. Domain and hosting accounts belong to the café. Final code and approved site assets transfer on full payment; third-party assets retain their licenses. Domain, hosting, provider subscriptions, processing, delivery, printing, photography, translation, advertising and ongoing content support are separate. No subscriptions are bought until the café selects and approves them. No custom ordering API, new POS, loyalty system or native mobile app in this scope.

**Recommended monthly retainer: $249/month for website care**, offered explicitly alongside the $2,500 build. Include two hours of small site edits (including request coordination/testing), routine website maintenance, uptime alerts reviewed during business hours, weekly menu/order-link checks, a monthly recovery-copy check, and a short monthly performance summary with one recommendation. Routine scheduled care/reporting is outside the two-hour edit allowance; recommendation implementation uses it. Unused edit time expires monthly. Keep POS/menu administration with the provider and café.

The optional plan starts 31 days after launch, is billed monthly in advance and has no minimum term. Cancel by email before renewal, with service through the paid period and no prorated refund. Target an initial email response within two business days, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern excluding U.S. federal holidays; this is not a repair-time guarantee or 24/7 coverage. Larger repairs, new features and overages require a separate written quote. Hosting/domain and provider charges remain separate. The client keeps its accounts/site and receives current handoff materials when care ends. Initial build corrections and the first results review are included even without the retainer. Full terms are in the editable proposal.

**Retainer economics:** Twelve paid months produce $2,988 in service revenue; build plus those 12 months totals $5,488 before third-party charges. This is proposed pricing, not evidence of market rates or their willingness to pay. Budget about three total service hours per month, including two hours of edits and routine care/reporting: $249 ÷ 3 = $83/hour before overhead and tools. At five hours it falls to $49.80/hour. Reassess scope if ongoing effort regularly exceeds that budget; do not promise unlimited edits.

In the illustrative $20 pickup example above, $249 ÷ $0.32 requires about **779 shifted pickup orders per month just to cover the retainer**, before hosting/provider costs or build recovery. Sell the ongoing service for upkeep and useful reporting, with fee savings measured honestly. Do not claim the retainer pays for itself without their numbers.

Estimate delivery effort before presenting the $2,500 build price. For example, at 25 total hours it yields $100/hour before overhead and any included costs; at 40 hours it yields $62.50/hour. These are internal planning calculations.

**Smaller fallback: $750 direct-ordering setup**, if the entire gap is activating/configuring an existing eligible storefront and placing its link on their profiles. Suggested scope: one location, existing menu, one handoff session, one QR design, no custom website or data migration. Native provider onboarding may already be included in their plan; disclose that option. Credit this fee toward the $2,500 build if they upgrade within 30 days, so total SKOX fees remain $2,500 for the combined scope. These are recommended draft terms, not commitments made to the café.

## Proposed page and customer flow

1. **First screen:** actual coffee/food photo, “Colombian coffee. Your next morning ritual.” as a draft headline, Winter Park location, Order Pickup and View Menu actions. The owner verifies the coffee claim and final brand voice.
2. **Menu preview:** a small selection of real, current favorites. Link directly to the provider menu; do not create a second full menu that silently becomes stale.
3. **The café story:** concise, owner-approved sourcing and people details. Real imagery with permission.
4. **Visit:** confirmed address, current hours, phone, directions and clear pickup instructions. Avoid inferred parking or seating promises.
5. **Closing action:** Order Pickup. Delivery alternatives remain available where useful, labeled by provider.

On a phone, ordering stays easy to reach without covering content. Use readable type, strong contrast, visible focus, compressed imagery and immediate access to menu/hours. No entrance animation should delay ordering. The café’s visual identity should guide the palette; SKOX’s Steep reference is not automatically their brand.

Intended journey: Google/Instagram/QR → café website → provider checkout → kitchen ticket → pickup. An outbound click is not a completed order. Confirm whether the provider supports purchase attribution; otherwise report website clicks separately from provider order totals.

## Discovery call and acceptance criteria

Ask in this order:

1. Who currently handles ownership/management, online ordering and website decisions? What is their preferred business email and contracting entity?
2. Is Toast the current POS? Is online ordering already included or enabled? Is there an existing direct link, former website or domain account?
3. What are the last 30 days of orders, average ticket, refunds, promotions and net fees, split into counter, marketplace pickup and delivery? Ask for totals or redacted statements, not customer-level data.
4. What would make this useful: fewer calls, more breakfast pickup, clearer store info, larger orders, or something else? Can the team handle additional pickup demand?
5. Which items, modifiers, prep times, sold-out handling and pickup instructions need to work? Who maintains hours and menu data?
6. Is the proposed investment sensible? Who can supply photos/logo/copy, approve revisions and grant account invitations?

Before launch: owner-approved content and contact details; mobile/desktop and keyboard checks; working map/menu/order links; configured sold-out/closed-hours behavior; owner-authorized test orders confirming modifier, price, payment, receipt and kitchen routing; agreed refund handling; measurement checked; domain/account handoff completed. A provider delay prevents promising a live ordering date.

For the 30-day review: compare completed direct pickup orders, channel mix, actual net fees, fulfillment issues and additional fixed costs with a comparable baseline. Report seasonality/promotions and attribution gaps. Judge business value using contribution, not website traffic or gross revenue alone. Start with store signage and existing profiles before recommending paid advertising.

## Outreach approach

Use the short email in the companion draft. The first ask is a 15-minute conversation with the right person. Personalize with the real visit; do not invent a drink purchased, staff conversation or relationship. Send the proposal after the contact is confirmed or as a relevant attachment, not the internal ownership/fee research.

Do not claim they lose 30% on every order, need to abandon delivery apps, have a broken Toast account, or will recover the fee in a month. Do not mention the dissolved historical LLC in a sales email. Keep the offer practical: find the existing direct option, make it easy to discover, and build the site if it solves a real need.

Deliverables prepared: research brief, editable proposal, four-page proposal PDF including monthly care and a menu-based value example, menu price CSV and reproducible calculation, initial email, one follow-up and an in-person opener. No website, account setting, order, message or public listing has been changed for this prospect.
