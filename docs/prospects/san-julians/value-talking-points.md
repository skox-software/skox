# San Julián’s: evidence for the value conversation

Researched September 9, 2026. Use industry findings to explain the opportunity; use the café’s own numbers to estimate value. These sources do not measure San Julián’s lost orders or prove that a new website will increase sales.

Updated with [their published menu analysis](menu-analysis.md): 54 visible base-priced listings average **$5.90 per item**. This is not average order value. The meeting example below now uses their **$15.65 latte-and-breakfast-arepa basket**, replacing the earlier generic $15 ticket assumption. Public marketplace prices need confirmation against direct/in-store prices.

## Three findings worth discussing

| Evidence | Source and population | Practical interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 85% of diners look at a new restaurant’s menu online most of the time; 80% always/often check its website. | [TouchBistro 2024 American Diner Trends](https://www.touchbistro.com/blog/2024-diner-trends-report/). Survey of 1,500 U.S. diners, fielded September 12–21, 2023 with Maru/Matchbox. Older evidence; do not label it a 2026 survey. | A useful website can help someone evaluate the café before visiting. Existing marketplace menus already meet some of this need; absence of an owned website does not imply losing 80% of customers. |
| 47% of adults pick up takeout from restaurants, coffee shops, snack places or delis at least weekly. | [National Restaurant Association, 2025 Off-Premises Restaurant Trends](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/research-reports/off-premises-restaurant-trends-2025/), published April 15, 2025. Public summary; sample size and detailed methodology are not supplied on that page. | Pickup is an established behavior in a category that explicitly includes coffee shops. The figure includes phone and counter ordering, not just online orders. |
| 43% cited location/convenience as a factor in deciding to visit a newly discovered restaurant. | [Toast, How Guests Discover New Restaurants in 2026](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/how-guests-discover-new-restaurants), published July 30, 2026, based on an October 2025 U.S. diner survey. | Clear location, hours and ordering information support a decision customers already care about. This is stated preference, not a measured website conversion lift. |

Toast’s article inconsistently reports 1,466 respondents in the introduction and 1,446 in its methodology. Avoid citing an exact sample size without clarification. Its discovery channels and decision factors may overlap; do not add their percentages. TouchBistro and Toast sell restaurant technology, so their findings are vendor-sponsored evidence, not independent tests of this proposal.

## Fees: where value must be measured carefully

DoorDash’s advertised marketplace commissions are 15%, 25% or 30% for delivery and 6% for eligible pickup. Their actual contract is unknown. [DoorDash marketplace pricing](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/marketplace/how-it-works-for-restaurants).

At an assumed $5,000/month in marketplace delivery sales, those advertised rates imply $750–$1,500/month in commission. That is a cost illustration, not verified café spending or recoverable savings: it includes services such as delivery/customer acquisition, and direct delivery still incurs costs. A website does not make every marketplace order transferable.

For pickup, applying 6% marketplace commission and hypothetical direct processing at 2.9% + $0.30 to the $15.65 menu-based basket gives a difference of $0.18515/order before settlement rounding and recurring costs. At 300 shifted orders/month the unrounded model gives $55.55, which does not cover a $249 retainer by itself. This is not their verified DoorDash basket price or Toast processing quote. Confirm channel prices, processor quotes and the same fulfillment method before comparing channels.

## Explain the opportunity in orders per day

Published item prices: **Latte $4.70 + Breakfast Egg Arepa $10.95 = $15.65**. This is an example basket, not a verified average ticket or advertised bundle. [San Julián’s Uber Eats menu](https://www.ubereats.com/store/san-julians-coffee-bar/DWgSDzGPX5yh5H5sigTAWw).

Assume the same subtotal for direct pickup, 26 trading days/month, and 40% contribution after ingredients, packaging, transaction fees and incremental labor. That leaves $6.26 from each truly additional basket before the SKOX fee and other new fixed costs. The café’s actual direct prices, trading days and contribution are unknown.

| Truly additional orders per trading day | Additional monthly sales | Contribution before SKOX care | After $249/month care |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | $1,220.70 | $488.28 | $239.28 |
| 5 | $2,034.50 | $813.80 | $564.80 |
| 8 | $3,255.20 | $1,302.08 | $1,053.08 |

The last column excludes the $2,500 build and any additional hosting/provider costs. It is not net business profit. Existing counter or marketplace orders merely moving online are not additional sales and must be assessed separately for fee changes. These scenarios do not predict website results.

For a budgeting comparison, spread the $2,500 build across 12 months ($208.33/month) and add the $249 care fee: $457.33/month before other costs. At $6.26 contribution per additional breakfast basket, 74 additional baskets/month, or roughly three per trading day, would cover those amounts. This is a required performance threshold, not a claim that the project will achieve it; it is not the upfront cash payment schedule.

Basket mix matters: at the same assumed 40% contribution, five additional $4.70 lattes/day across 26 days produce only $244.40 contribution, or **-$4.60 after care**, before build/other fixed costs. The breakfast example must not be presented as applying to coffee-only transactions.

## Say it like this

“I looked into how people choose places to eat. One U.S. diner study found that 85% check the menu online before trying somewhere new, and the Restaurant Association found that nearly half of adults pick up takeout at least weekly. I think there’s an opportunity to make it easier for those people to find your menu and order directly.

“I used your published menu to make this concrete: a latte and breakfast egg arepa add up to $15.65 on Uber Eats. If easier ordering brought five extra breakfast orders a day across 26 days, that would be about $2,035 in monthly sales. At an assumed 40% left after those orders’ costs, that would leave about $814 before website costs. I’d want to check your direct prices and actual margins to see what would make the investment worthwhile.”

Those industry claims are supported by the TouchBistro and National Restaurant Association sources above. The financial figures are arithmetic from explicitly assumed inputs.

Then ask for their actual average ticket, trading days, contribution per order, channel mix, fees and pickup capacity. Agree what to measure before launching. Sell the care plan for real upkeep, timely edits and reporting; it does not include campaigns or guarantee additional orders.
