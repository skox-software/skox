## How to run it

**Ask open questions first. Compare her answers against the record. Do not
lead with the record.** You are not hiding anything — checking a state
registry before contracting is normal and you can say so if it comes up. But
an open question gives her the chance to tell you the innocent version
herself, and how she tells it is the information.

Do not do any free work on the call. No live scoping, no "I'll just send you a
quick mockup", no free strategy. You have done enough of that.

## The six must-ask questions

### 1. The entity
> "Before I write anything up — which company would we be contracting with?"

- **Good:** Credit Snitch LLC, said plainly.
- **Listen for:** hesitation, a different entity, "my partner's company", or a
  new LLC that does not exist yet.

### 2. The investors
> "You mentioned investors. How is that structured, and do they have any say in
> the product or any rights to it?"

- **Good:** specific. Friends and family, a note, a SAFE, a percentage. Who
  they are in broad terms. Clear that she owns the product decisions.
- **Red:** vague, or anything resembling *"once my investors see it, the money
  comes in."* If your build is what unlocks her funding, you are financing her
  raise. Walk.
- **Also listen for:** whether investors were told a product already exists.

### 3. The previous $20–30k
> "You said you've put $20–30k into this over three years. Who did you work
> with, what did you get, and what went wrong?"

Follow-up: *"Do you still have any of that — code, designs, accounts?"*

- **Good:** names the vendors, takes some responsibility ("I didn't know what to
  ask for"), has artifacts.
- **Red:** blames everyone, can't name who, nothing to show. You would be the
  next name on that list.

### 4. Compliance
> "Who handles your compliance — CROA and Florida's credit services rules? I've
> got three design questions I'd want to send them before building."

Follow-up: *"Do you carry the Florida credit services bond and trust account?"*
Florida §817.7005 bars a credit services organization from taking payment
before services are fully performed unless it has a $10,000 surety bond and a
Florida trust account holding the money until performance is complete.
Chapter 817 sits in Florida's criminal code.

- **Good:** names an attorney. Knows about the bond, or how her billing avoids
  needing it.
- **Red:** "What's CROA?" isn't disqualifying on its own. Indifference to
  finding out is.

### 5. References
> "Could you connect me with two Realtors who refer to you? I want to hear how
> the monthly updates work from their side before I design that part."

Framed as product research, which it genuinely is. It is also your reference
check on testimonials you otherwise cannot verify.

- **Red:** reluctance, or only offering Winston Christie.

### 6. The commitment
> "The way I'd start is two weeks of paid discovery at $4,500, fully credited
> to the build if you go ahead within 60 days. It ends with a fixed price and a
> fixed date. Does that work for you?"

- **Yes:** you have a commercial relationship. Proceed.
- **"Can you do it free / after investors / for equity?"** That is your answer.

## Full question list

### Her operation
- How many active clients do you have right now?
- How many could the team carry before it gets tight?
- How do you bill clients, and at what point in the month?
- Who produces the monthly Realtor updates, and how long does one take?
- What do clients actually do in the Credit Repair Cloud portal today?
- Which Credit Repair Cloud plan are you on?
- Do your Realtors have affiliate logins there, and do they use them?
- Roughly what share of clients come from Realtors versus directly?
- Roughly how many active SmartCredit members are on your affiliate link?

### History and claims
Ask these openly. They are fair questions from someone who will be putting her
claims into a product.

- *"When did Credit Snitch start? Was there an earlier company before the LLC?"*
  The record shows the domain in 2022 and the LLC in 2023. A Spring Valley, NY
  business called Soto Worldwide Holdings, which describes itself as financial
  literacy and **credit restoration**, appears to be an earlier venture.
- *"I've seen the conversion figure as $24M, $27M, and higher on LinkedIn.
  Which do you stand behind, and how is it counted?"* Frame it as: *"If it goes
  in the product or on a landing page, I need the one you can document."*
- *"Which financial literacy certification do you hold?"* Verify it afterwards
  (see self-checks).
- *"For the DTI program — who actually makes those loans, and under what
  license? And is the 15–20% annual, or for the 30–60 day term?"* If it is
  per term, the annualised rate is roughly 90–240%.

### Relationships
- *"Do you set up LLCs for clients as part of the business credit program?"*
  Ask this one. It is the likely innocent explanation for several of the
  entities, and asking lets her give it.
- *"How do you know Winston Christie?"* Open. Then listen. If she volunteers the
  business partnership, fine. If she describes him only as a happy partner
  Realtor, note it. A testimonial from someone with a business connection is
  supposed to disclose that connection under FTC endorsement rules. That is her
  counsel's issue, but it matters if the app ever displays testimonials.

### The product
- What does success look like 90 days after launch?
- Who on your team would use the Command Center every day?
- Does the app have to replace the Credit Repair Cloud client portal?
- Spanish at launch, or after?
- What budget range and timing did you have in mind?

### Decision
- *"Who else needs to approve this — you alone, or your investors too?"*
- *"What would stop you starting this month?"*

## Walk-away signals

Any one of these should end it, politely:

- Won't pay for discovery.
- Payment contingent on investors seeing it first.
- Equity, revenue share, or deferred payment instead of cash.
- Can't or won't say which entity contracts, or how investors are involved.
- Asks you to build dispute letter generation, billing that charges before work
  is done, or anything touching the DTI loans.
- No attorney, and no interest in getting one.
- Won't provide a single reference you can call.

## Don't

- Open with the filings.
- Accuse her of anything. Ask, and listen.
- Agree to a price below $22,000 for the beta build. That's your floor.
- Promise a date before discovery.
- Leave without a clear next step and who owns it.

## Five-minute self-checks before the call

1. **LinkedIn** (you're logged in): her profile at
   linkedin.com/in/angelique-soto-0615128. Check the $87M and $100M figures, and
   whether it mentions **EB-5**, which one search snippet suggested. EB-5 is a
   regulated immigrant-investor program. If it is there and she has
   "investors", ask how the two relate.
2. **Her certification**, once she names it: the NFEC has a verification tool
   for the CFEI designation at financialeducatorscouncil.org.
3. **NMLS Consumer Access** (nmlsconsumeraccess.org): search her name. Tells you
   whether she holds a mortgage loan originator license, which matters for the
   DTI program and for a third-party listing that puts "Mpire Mortgage" in her
   history.
4. **Florida OFR license search**: for whoever she says originates the DTI loans.
