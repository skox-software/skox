**Window:** Oct 15, 2026 → Jan 12, 2027 (Day 30 = Nov 13, Day 60 = Dec 13, Day 90 = Jan 12).
**Owners:** Josh (sales, operations, client relationship) and Reiss (build, product).
**Built from:** the offer masterclass (`docs/ads/masterclass/2026-10-14-summary.md`), the week-1 ad and recording data (`docs/ads/reviews/2026-10-11-google.md`), the competitor research (`docs/ads/research/competitors-2026-10-14.md`) and Josh's pricing decisions (`docs/ads/economics.md`).

## Where we are, honestly

- **The offer is real and priced:** focused builds from $5,000, connected systems $10–20k, care plan ~$250/month, a fixed price and date in writing, and you own the code.
- **Every sale so far came through a relationship** (Lorenzen, Pat Ireland, Credit Snitch). Google Ads has produced 10 clicks and 0 leads, and at $300/month it can produce 1–3 leads a month at best.
- **There's no proof yet:** no case studies, no testimonials. That's the single biggest thing holding back every channel.

**So the next 90 days aren't about scale. They're about three founding clients, three case studies, and one repeatable package.** Scale comes after that, and it's much easier once you have it.

## Non-negotiables (from your own rules)

1. **Sell the outcome, never the method.** Don't say "AI" or "vibe coding" in outreach, proposals or calls. Say what the owner gets: "your quotes go out in minutes with your margins built in".
2. **No invented proof.** Use the live demos (they're labeled as concepts) and your process. Never imply you've built for someone in their trade until you have.
3. **Never name a client without written permission.** That includes Lorenzen and Pat.
4. **Price is a floor, not a discount.** The public price stays "from $5,000". Founding clients get *more*, not a lower price (see the offer below).
5. **Stay legal:**
   - Cold email must have a real business address and an easy opt-out (CAN-SPAM).
   - **Never cold-text** someone who didn't give you their number (TCPA). Calls, email, LinkedIn and in-person are fine.
   - Texting is fine once they've texted you or asked you to.

## Days 1–30: conversations, not pitches (Oct 15 → Nov 13)

**Goal:** 15–20 discovery calls. **Exit test:** 7+ owners describe the same problem in similar words, and 3+ say some version of "if you build that, tell me".

**Weekly rhythm:**
- **Mon:** warm intro asks (10).
- **Tue and Thu:** cold emails (15) plus LinkedIn (10).
- **Wed and Fri:** follow-ups and calls.
- **Each week:** 2–4 discovery calls.
- **Sun:** 30-minute review of the numbers.

### Script 1: Warm intro ask (text or email to someone you know)

> Hey [Name], quick favor. Reiss and I are building software for [roofing companies] — things like quote builders and job-costing tools, built around how a company already works. I'm trying to talk to 10 owners this month just to learn how they run things. Not selling anything on the call.
>
> Do you know anyone who owns a [roofing / landscaping] company I could buy a coffee for? A one-line intro is plenty. Appreciate you.

### Script 2: Warm DM to an owner you know

> Hey [Name], hope business is good. Reiss and I are building software for [trade] companies and I'd value 20 minutes of your honest take on how you handle [quotes / scheduling / job costs] today. Not a pitch. I'm trying to learn where the real headaches are. Coffee's on me this week or next?

### Script 3: Cold email, first touch (owner-run company)

Subject options: `quick question about [Company]'s quotes` / `[First name], how long does a quote take you?`

> Hi [First name],
>
> I'm Josh, co-founder of SKOX. We build software for [roofing] companies, things like a quote builder that uses your own prices, pitch and waste rules and sends a good/better/best quote the customer can approve from a text.
>
> I'm not reaching out to sell you anything yet. I'm talking to 10 [roofing] owners in Central Florida this month to learn how quoting actually works for them. 20 minutes, on the phone or over coffee.
>
> Would [Tuesday or Thursday] work?
>
> Josh Fox
> SKOX · skoxsoftware.com
> [business mailing address]
> Not interested? Just reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.

### Script 4: Cold email follow-up (4 days later, same thread)

> Hi [First name], bumping this in case it got buried. If quoting isn't a headache for you, I'd still love to know what is. Even a one-line reply helps.
>
> If you'd rather see what I mean first: [skoxsoftware.com/for/roofing]. It has a working example you can try, with sample data.
>
> Josh

### Script 5: Cold email, last touch (7 days after #4)

> Hi [First name], last note from me. If the timing's ever right to talk about [quotes / scheduling / job costing], I'm at [phone]. Wishing you a strong season.
>
> Josh

### Script 6: LinkedIn connection note (≤300 characters)

> Hi [Name], I'm a co-founder at SKOX. We build software for [roofing] companies. I'm learning how owners handle [quoting] and would value your take. Happy to connect either way.

LinkedIn follow-up after they accept:

> Thanks for connecting. Quick one: how does [Company] put together quotes today? Spreadsheet, an app, or in someone's head? Asking because it's the most common bottleneck I hear about.

### Script 7: Cold call opener (30 seconds)

> Hi, is this [Name]? This is Josh with SKOX. I'll be quick. We build software for [roofing] companies, and I'm calling owners to learn how they handle [quotes] today, not to sell anything. Do you have two minutes, or is there a better time this week?
>
> *(If yes)* How does a quote get from the measurement to the customer right now? … What's the most annoying part of that? … If that part just worked, what would it be worth to you?
>
> *(Close)* This is really helpful. Could I grab 20 minutes later this week to go deeper? I'll show you a working example of what we've built for this.

### Script 8: The discovery call (20–30 minutes)

Don't pitch until minute 20, and only if they ask.

1. **Context (2 min):** "Tell me about the company. How many people, crews, locations? What does a normal week look like?"
2. **The process (8 min):** "Walk me through how [a quote / a day's schedule / a job's costs] works today, step by step. Who touches it? What tools?"
3. **The pain (5 min):**
   - "Where does that break?"
   - "When it breaks, what does it cost you, in time, money or missed jobs?"
   - "How often?"
4. **What they've tried (3 min):**
   - "What have you tried to fix it?"
   - "What did you like or hate about it?"
   - *(Listen for the subscription apps they pay for, and what those don't do.)*
5. **Priority (2 min):** "If you could fix one thing in the business by spring, would it be this?"
6. **If they ask what you do:** show the trade page demo for 3 minutes. Then: "Yours would be built around exactly what you just told me: your prices, your crews, your tools. It works alongside your current software if it allows a connection, or replaces it if it's holding you back."
7. **Close:** "Can I send you a short summary of what I heard? And if we end up building something like this, would you want to be one of the first three?"

**After every call, log in the pipeline:** their words for the pain (quote them), current tools, rough cost of the problem, company size, interest level (cold / warm / hot).

## Days 31–60: the founding-client offer (Nov 14 → Dec 13)

**Goal:** 3 founding clients signed with deposits paid. **Exit test:** 3 signed scopes. Builds for #1 and #2 started.

### The founding-client offer (3 spots, per niche or overall)

Keep the price. Add value instead of cutting it:

| What they get | Normal | Founding client |
|---|---|---|
| Focused build | From $5,000 | **Same price** |
| Care plan | ~$250/month | **First 3 months free**, then ~$250/month (optional) |
| Priority | Normal queue | **Built first**, with a weekly check-in |
| Their input | — | **They shape the product** that other companies in their trade will use |
| What we ask | — | **A case study with their name and results** after launch, and **2 introductions** if they're happy |

Why this works: the public price holds, and the cost to you is about $750 of care plan each. The case study and the intros are worth far more than a discount.

### Script 9: Founding-client offer (to a warm "if you build that" owner)

> Hi [Name], thanks again for walking me through how [quotes] work at [Company]. We're picking three [roofing] companies to be founding clients for the [quote builder].
>
> What that means: a focused build around your prices and process, at our normal fixed price (from $5,000; yours would be in writing before you pay anything), built first, plus three months of care and updates free. In return we'd ask for a short case study once it's live, and two intros if you're happy with it.
>
> Want to grab 30 minutes this week so I can put the written scope together for you?

### Script 10: The written proposal (one page, three options)

This is the selling-and-pricing panel's recommended structure. Keep it to one page, plain language, no jargon.

> **[Company]: [Quote builder] proposal**
>
> **What you told us:** [2–3 sentences in their words: the problem and what it costs.]
>
> **What we'll build:** [3–5 bullets of what the owner will be able to do, not features.]
>
> | | Focused | **Recommended** | Full |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Includes | [core tool] | [core tool + the one connection that matters] | [connected system] |
> | Fixed price | $[5,000+] | $[X] | $[Y] |
> | Delivery date | [date] | [date] | [date] |
>
> **Care plan (optional):** hosting, updates, small changes and support, ~$250/month, cancel anytime. Founding clients: first 3 months free.
> **Payment:** [deposit %] to start, the rest at launch. *(Josh: set the deposit; Lorenzen was 50%.)*
> **You own it:** the code, the accounts and the data are yours.
> **Halfway review:** you'll use a working version at the midpoint.
> **Founding-client terms:** a short case study after launch, and two introductions if you're happy.

### Script 11: Proposal follow-up (3 days later)

> Hi [Name], checking the proposal landed. Any questions on the options? Most people in your spot pick [Recommended] because [one-line reason tied to what they said]. Happy to adjust the scope if something's missing.

### Objection handling

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "That's more than I pay for [app] a month." | "Right. You're comparing one month to forever. At [X users × $Y] you'll pay about $[Z] over five years and still not own it. This is a one-time price, built around how you work, and you own it. There's a rent-vs-own calculator on our page if you want to run your own numbers." |
| "I already use [app]." | "Keep it. If it allows a connection, we build the piece it doesn't do and plug it in. We only replace it if it's holding you back." |
| "What if it doesn't work the way we need?" | "You get the price and the date in writing before you pay, and you use a working version halfway through, while there's still time to change things." |
| "We're too busy right now." | "Totally fair. That's usually the reason to fix it. Want me to hold a founding spot until [month]? We'll take three, and then the founding terms close." *(Only say this while it's true.)* |
| "Who else have you built this for?" | "You'd be one of our first three in [roofing], which is why the founding terms exist. Here's what we've built in general [demo links], and you'd work directly with the two of us who build it." |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | "The price is fixed for what's in the scope. We can make the scope smaller: here's what the Focused option drops." |

## Days 61–90: deliver, prove, package (Dec 14 → Jan 12)

**Goal:** deliver founding builds on the dates promised, turn them into proof, and name the package. Late December is slow for outreach, so use it to build and write.

1. **Deliver.** Hit every date. A missed date costs more than any feature adds.
2. **Run the halfway review** with each client. Record what they say in their own words; that becomes your copy.
3. **Write case studies** (with permission). One page each:
   - **Before:** their words.
   - **What we built.**
   - **After:** time saved, quotes per week, errors caught. Their numbers, never invented.
   - **A quote from the owner.**
4. **Ask for referrals** (Script 13).
5. **Name the package.** If 2 of 3 builds share the same core, that's your product. For example: *"SKOX Quote Desk for roofing companies: your prices, good/better/best quotes, approval and deposit by text. From $5,000, live in [X] weeks."* Put it on the trade page. Point the ads at it.
6. **Update the site:** a case-study section on the matching trade page, and raise the floor if demand supports it.

### Script 12: Case study request (at launch, when they're happy)

> [Name], it's been great building this with you. As part of the founding terms, could we write up a short case study? It'd cover where you started, what we built and what's changed, in your words. I'll draft it and you approve every line before anything goes public.

### Script 13: Referral ask (2–4 weeks after launch)

> [Name], glad [the quote builder] is earning its keep. Quick ask: who are two other [roofing] owners you respect who'd want the same thing? A text intro is perfect. I'll take great care of them.

### Script 14: Care plan conversion (end of the free 3 months)

> Hi [Name], your three free months of care wrap up on [date]. Most clients keep it: hosting, updates, fixes and small changes for ~$250/month, cancel anytime. Want me to keep it running? If not, I'll hand everything over so your team can manage it.

## Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Sign | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Can't get meetings | <5 calls by Day 21 | Switch to the niche with better warm access. Go in person. |
| Calls happen but no one wants it | No "if you build that" by Day 30 | The problem isn't painful enough. Try the second niche's main problem. |
| Builds take longer than priced | Hours > ~60 per $5k build | Narrow the scope, raise the floor to $7,500, reuse more code. |
| Founding clients go quiet | No reply for 7+ days | Call, don't email. Founders answer phones. |
| Burnout with the day job | Missed weekly targets 2 weeks running | Cut outreach volume, never cut follow-ups or delivery quality. |
