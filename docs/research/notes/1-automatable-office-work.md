# How much US small-business office work is still manual, repetitive and automatable (2022–2026)

Scope note: US focus, firms of 5–50 employees. Almost nothing in the literature is cut at exactly that size band; every figure below says what population it actually covers. Grading: **STRONG** = government, peer-reviewed/academic, or large-sample with published method; **WEAK** = vendor-commissioned, small-sample, or marketing. Where only a secondary source could be reached, that is stated. Figures not found are listed under Gaps, not invented.

Access notes for the report writer: BLS OEWS pages block automated fetching (returned "Access Denied"); the OEWS-based counts below were taken from O*NET's "Wages & Employment" panel, which republishes BLS data, and from the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook via search. The Goldman Sachs PDF, Eloundou et al. PDF, Frey & Osborne PDF and the AMA 2025 PA survey PDF were downloaded and read in full text. Clio's report pages and Brookings returned 403 to fetching; their figures are from Clio's/Brookings' own coverage as relayed by secondary pages and are flagged.

## Key Question 1: What share of work time or tasks in administrative/clerical occupations is estimated to be automatable?

### Takeaway
Every major estimate since 2013 puts office/administrative support at or near the top of the automatability ranking: Goldman Sachs (2023) says 46% of office-and-admin-support tasks could be done by generative AI; McKinsey (2017) says ~64–69% of time spent collecting and processing data is automatable with demonstrated tech; Frey & Osborne (2013) gave 0.96–0.99 probabilities for secretaries, bookkeepers, data-entry keyers and receptionists. Eloundou et al. (2023) and Pew (2023) agree clerical roles sit in the most-exposed quartile. All of these are *technical potential* or *exposure* measures, not observed automation, and none is cut by firm size.

### Cited Findings

**Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research, "The Potentially Large Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Economic Growth" (Briggs, Kodnani, Hatzius, Pierdomenico), 26 March 2023 — STRONG (large-sample, O*NET/OEWS-based; investment-bank research, not peer-reviewed)**
- "roughly two-thirds of US occupations are exposed to some degree of automation by AI, and that of those occupations which are exposed, most have a significant—but partial—share of their workload (25-50%) that can be replaced." Employment-weighted using OEWS: "one-fourth of current work tasks could be automated by AI in the US (Exhibit 5, top panel), with particularly high exposures in administrative (46%) and legal (44%) professions and low exposures in physically-intensive professions such as construction (6%) and maintenance (4%)." Exhibit 5 (US) labels the 46% bar "Office and Administrative Support"; next bars are Legal 44, Architecture & Engineering 37, Life/Physical/Social Science 36, Business & Financial Operations 35, Community & Social Service 33. — [Goldman Sachs PDF](https://www.gspublishing.com/content/research/en/reports/2023/03/27/d64e052b-0f6e-45d7-967b-d7be35fabd16.pdf)
- Method: O*NET task "importance" and "complexity" thresholds per occupation; occupations with substantial outdoor/physical time assumed non-automatable. Population: all US employment (OEWS weights). Caution: this is *share of tasks AI could perform*, for the whole occupation group nationally — it says nothing about small firms specifically and nothing about what is actually automated today. The widely repeated "300 million jobs" line in press coverage is a different, global, full-time-equivalent extrapolation from the same paper.

**McKinsey Global Institute, "A future that works: Automation, employment, and productivity" (Jan 2017) — STRONG (large-scale activity analysis of ~2,000 activities across ~800 US occupations; consultancy, not peer-reviewed)**
- Technical automation potential by activity (share of time spent on the activity that could be automated by adapting currently demonstrated technology): processing data ~69%; collecting data ~64%; predictable physical work ~81%. Collecting + processing data + predictable physical work "make up 51 percent of activities in the [US] economy", i.e. ~$2.7 trillion in wages. "Less than 5 percent of all occupations can be automated entirely", while "about 60 percent of all occupations have at least 30 percent of constituent activities that could be automated." — [MGI executive summary (PDF)](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/featured%20insights/digital%20disruption/harnessing%20automation%20for%20a%20future%20that%20works/mgi-a-future-that-works_executive-summary.pdf); [MGI in-brief](https://mckinsey.com/~/media/McKinsey/Featured%20Insights/Digital%20Disruption/Harnessing%20automation%20for%20a%20future%20that%20works/MGI-A-future-that-works_In-brief.ashx)
- Caution: 2017, pre-generative-AI; "technical potential" explicitly excludes cost, labor-market and adoption factors. The activity-level numbers (64%/69%) are for *all* workers' data-collection/processing time, not clerical occupations alone — though clerical roles are where that time concentrates. The writer should not label 64–69% as "share of a bookkeeper's job".

**McKinsey, "The economic potential of generative AI: The next productivity frontier" (June 2023) — STRONG-ish (same MGI activity model; consultancy)**
- "current generative AI and other technologies have the potential to automate work activities that absorb 60 to 70 percent of employees' time today" (up from ~50% in MGI's prior estimate); midpoint for automating half of today's work activities moved roughly a decade earlier, to ~2045 in the midpoint scenario; 63 use cases across 16 business functions analysed. — [McKinsey report page](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier) (page timed out when fetched; wording confirmed via multiple outlets quoting the report, e.g. [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/trends/story/generative-ai-could-add-between-26-tn-and-44-tn-worth-of-annual-productivity-globally-mckinsey-385810-2023-06-15))
- Caution: "generative AI *and other technologies*" — not a gen-AI-only number; economy-wide, not clerical-specific; a potential, not a forecast.

**Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, Rock (OpenAI/UPenn), "GPTs are GPTs" (arXiv 2303.10130, v5 Aug 2023) — STRONG (academic working paper; O*NET task-level, human + GPT-4 ratings)**
- "around 80% of the U.S. workforce could have at least 10% of their work tasks affected by the introduction of LLMs, while approximately 19% of workers may see at least 50% of their tasks impacted." Average occupation ~15% of tasks directly exposed (α); "over 30% for β and surpasses 50% for ζ" (β/ζ = with LLM-powered software). Exposure is defined as cutting task time by ≥50% at equal quality, *not* full automation. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130); [PDF v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10130v5)
- Table 4 (highest-exposure occupations): Human ζ 100%: Mathematicians, **Tax Preparers**, Financial Quantitative Analysts, Writers and Authors, Web and Digital Interface Designers ("Humans labeled 15 occupations as 'fully exposed'"). Model α/β: **Correspondence Clerks 95.2%**, Court Reporters 92.9–96.4%, Proofreaders 90.9–95.5%. Model ζ 100%: Accountants and Auditors, **Legal Secretaries and Administrative Assistants**, Clinical Data Managers, etc. ("The model labeled 86 occupations as 'fully exposed'"). — same PDF, Table 4.
- Caution: the paper's tables list only the top-5 per measure; the exact scores for 43-3031 bookkeeping clerks, 43-6014 secretaries, 43-9021 data entry keyers are in the authors' released dataset, not in the PDF (see Gaps). Exposure ≠ automatable; the authors stress "it does not necessarily suggest that their tasks can be fully automated."

**Pew Research Center, "Which U.S. Workers Are More Exposed to AI on Their Jobs?" (Kochhar, 26 July 2023) — STRONG (government data: O*NET 27.3 + CPS 2022; plus ATP survey n=11,004)**
- "19% of American workers were in jobs that are the most exposed to AI" in 2022 (top-quartile of 873 O*NET occupations by high-exposure work activities, 41 activities rated); 23% least exposed. Named most-exposed examples include **budget analysts, data entry keyers**, tax preparers, technical writers, web developers. Most-exposed jobs skew toward higher-paying, college-educated, and women (21% of women vs 17% of men). — [Pew report](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/07/26/which-u-s-workers-are-more-exposed-to-ai-on-their-jobs/); [PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2023/07/st_2023.07.26_ai-and-jobs-1.pdf)
- Caution: Pew does not publish a figure for the "office and administrative support" group as a whole on that page; "exposed" means activities could be replaced *or assisted*; no firm-size cut.

**Frey & Osborne, "The Future of Employment" (Oxford Martin, Sept 2013; published 2017) — STRONG academic but HISTORICAL (2013; pre-LLM; occupation-level probabilities, widely criticised for treating whole occupations as units)**
- Probability of computerisation (rank of 702): Data Entry Keyers 0.99 (#691); Tax Preparers 0.99 (#695); Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers 0.99 (#701); Insurance Underwriters 0.99; Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks 0.98 (#671); Legal Secretaries 0.98; Procurement Clerks 0.98; Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks 0.98; Loan Officers 0.98; Payroll and Timekeeping Clerks 0.97; File Clerks 0.97; Real Estate Brokers 0.97; Secretaries and Administrative Assistants (except legal/medical/executive) 0.96 (#634); Receptionists and Information Clerks 0.96; Dispatchers (except police/fire/ambulance) 0.96 (#627); Office Clerks, General 0.96; Billing and Posting Clerks 0.96; Surveying and Mapping Technicians 0.96; Property, Real Estate and Community Association Managers 0.81; Medical Secretaries 0.81; Customer Service Representatives 0.55; Surveyors 0.38. Headline: 47% of US employment in the high-risk category. — [Oxford Martin PDF](https://oms-www.files.svdcdn.com/production/downloads/academic/The_Future_of_Employment.pdf)
- Caution: 2013 vintage, "could be automated over an unspecified number of years, perhaps a decade or two"; critiqued by OECD (Arntz et al. 2016) for overstating by scoring occupations rather than tasks. Use only with the year label.

**Brookings, "Generative AI, the American worker, and the future of work" (Kinder, de Souza Briggs, Muro, Liu, 17 Oct 2024) — STRONG (think-tank; builds on Eloundou scores + BLS)**
- Secondary summaries report: more than 30% of workers could see at least 50% of their tasks disrupted, with office/administrative support and business operations among the most exposed. — [Altiorem summary](https://altiorem.org/research/generative-ai-the-american-worker-and-the-future-of-work/); [BABL summary](https://babl.ai/generative-ais-potential-impact-on-american-workforce-sparks-urgent-call-for-preparedness/). Brookings' own page (https://www.brookings.edu/articles/generative-ais-impact-on-the-american-worker/) returned 403 when fetched. Caution: exact office/admin percentage not verified against the primary.

**Census Bureau / NBER, "Automation and the Workforce: A Firm-Level View from the 2019 Annual Business Survey" (Acemoglu, Anderson, Beede, Buffington, Childress, Dinlersoz, Foster, Goldschlag, Haltiwanger, Kroff, Restrepo, Zolas; Nov 2022, CES-WP-22-12 / NBER 30659) — STRONG (government; >300,000 firms)**
- Adoption of five advanced technologies (AI, robotics, dedicated equipment, specialized software, cloud) "remains low (especially for AI and robotics), varies substantially across industries, and concentrates on large and young firms"; 12–64% of US workers estimated to be exposed to these technologies depending on measure. — [Census working paper](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2022/adrm/CES-WP-22-12.html); [MIT PDF](https://shapingwork.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Acemoglu-et-al-Nov-2022.pdf)
- Caution: 2019 reference year; shows the *gap* between potential and actual adoption in small firms rather than a clerical-specific share.

### Inferences
- Across five independent methods spanning 2013–2024, clerical/administrative support is consistently in the top two or three occupation groups for automatability or AI exposure; the defensible range for "share of office/admin tasks that current technology could do" is roughly 45% (Goldman, gen-AI only, 2023) to ~65–70% (MGI, all demonstrated automation, data collection/processing activities).
- The Census ABS evidence (low actual adoption, concentrated in large firms) plus BTOS (see Q2) implies that in 5–50-person firms almost all of that technical potential is still unrealised, i.e. the work is still done by hand.
- None of these studies supports a sentence like "X% of a small-business bookkeeper's week is wasted"; they support "X% of the tasks in this occupation are the kind current software can do."

### Gaps
- Occupation-level Eloundou scores for 43-3031, 43-6014, 43-9021, 43-4171, 43-5032, 43-4051 are not printed in the paper; they are in the authors' GitHub data release, which was not fetched. Not found in this pass.
- McKinsey 2017 occupation-group figure for "office and administrative support" specifically: not found (the executive summary gives activity-level, not occupation-group, numbers; the PDF download failed).
- Goldman's per-detailed-occupation numbers (e.g., bookkeeping clerks) are not in the public PDF; only the 22-group bar chart.
- No study found that estimates automatability *by firm size*.

## Key Question 2: How many hours per week do small-business owners and staff report spending on admin/paperwork? (owner time vs staff time)

### Takeaway
Owner-time surveys cluster around one-fifth to one-third of the working week on admin: 36% of a 45.5-hour week (Time etc/Censuswide, n=251, 2023, WEAK), 22.4% of owner time on admin+legal+compliance for ≤5-employee firms (UENI, n=837, 2024, WEAK), 120 working days/year for SMBs across 11 countries (Sage/Plum, 2017, WEAK/dated). The only government time-use measure that touches this is Census BTOS, which measures AI *adoption* (not hours): ~5–10% of small firms used AI in production during 2024–2025. No credible US survey of *staff* (non-owner) admin hours in 5–50-person firms was found.

### Cited Findings

**Owner time**
- Time etc (virtual-assistant vendor) / Censuswide, 251 US entrepreneurs, fieldwork 22–28 Sept 2023 — **WEAK (vendor-commissioned, n=251, sells delegation)**: average work week 45.5 hours; 36% of the week on administrative tasks (≈16.4 h/week if multiplied — a derived figure, not reported by the survey); 29% work >50 h/week. Tasks done in a typical week: logging expenses 59%, research 49%, schedule management 45%, creating invoices 44%, data entry 43%, ordering supplies 40%, formatting documents 29%, chasing late payers 27%. — [Time etc article](https://timeetc.com/resources/how-to-achieve-more/the-big-price-of-small-tasks-how-entrepreneurs-may-be-unwittingly-keeping-their-businesses-small). Caution: "entrepreneurs in growth-oriented businesses", not a 5–50-employee frame; the sampled population is pre-disposed to think about delegation.
- UENI (website vendor) Research, 837 US businesses with ≤5 employees, fielded 6–13 May 2024 — **WEAK (vendor, micro-firms only)**: median 60 h/week; 62% work >50 h/week; administrative + legal + compliance = 22.4% of owner time on average, "79% higher than" the marketing/sales share. — [UENI research page](https://ueni.com/en-us/small-business-research/how-microbusiness-owners-spend-their-time). Caution: micro-firms (≤5), below the 5–50 band; admin not separated from compliance.
- Sage, "Sweating the Small Stuff: the impact of the bureaucracy burden" (Plum Consulting, >3,000 SMBs in 11 countries, Sept 2017) — **WEAK/dated (vendor; cross-country average)**: SMBs spend an average of 120 working days per year on administrative tasks (~5% of total manpower); US productivity loss estimated at 4.9% / $335.3bn; accounting is the largest admin task in the US at nearly a quarter of admin work. — [GlobeNewswire release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2017/09/25/1158001/0/en/Sage-survey-shows-that-unleashing-business-builders-from-burdensome-admin-could-unlock-over-C-728bn-in-lost-productivity.html); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/small-business/news/12367641/smbs-spend-120-working-days-per-year-on-admin-tasks-and-bookkeeping). Caution: 2017; "120 days" is a firm-level, not per-owner, figure and is an 11-country average; a secondary outlet quotes 230–240 days for the smallest firms, which could not be reconciled.
- Intuit QuickBooks (Oct 2022, US/CA/UK/AU) — **WEAK (vendor)**: businesses with an accountant/bookkeeper "save" ~73 working days per year in the US (97 CA, 59 UK, 71 AU). — [Datawrapper chart from the Intuit release](https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/9EeKf/1/). Caution: self-reported and framed to sell accounting services; sample size not shown on the chart.
- Cornerstone Advisors (fintech consultancy), 750 US small-business owners/executives, Q4 2023 — **WEAK (secondary only; original report not located)**: ~20.5 h/week on five accounting functions (invoicing 4.1 h, expense tracking 4.2 h, financial reporting 4.2 h; those three = 12.5 h). Only found via an aggregator ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-finance-administration-workload-statistics-2026)). Caution: do not cite without finding the Cornerstone original; could not be verified.
- American Action Forum (April 2017) — **WEAK-to-moderate (think-tank calculation from federal Paperwork Reduction Act inventories, not a survey)**: average small business (<50 employees) faces >379 hours/year of federal regulatory paperwork (~10 full-time weeks), from 3.3bn hours across ~8.9m small firms. — [AAF press release](https://www.americanactionforum.org/press-release/aaf-research-finds-small-businesses-disproportionately-impacted-regulations/); [AAF insight](https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/red-tape-burdens-small-businesses/). Caution: AAF itself notes burden-hour calculations are often called "artificial"; 2017; federal paperwork only (no state, no customer/vendor paperwork).
- Xero US guide "The hidden time tax" cites the 36% figure (via Forbes) and the AAF 379 hours; its own "15–20 admin hours a week" line is unsourced. — [Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/guides/time-tax-small-business/) — **WEAK**.

**Adoption of automation (proxy for how much is still manual) — STRONG (government)**
- US Census Bureau Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS), biweekly, ~1.2m firm sample frame; question: used AI "in producing goods or services" in the last two weeks. Research Matters blog (Dinlersoz & Goldschlag, 3 Dec 2024): Sept 2023–Aug 2024, firms with 250+ employees rose 5.2% → 7.8%; 1–4 employees 4.6% → 5.8%; **5–9, 10–19, 20–49 and 50–99 employee classes sat around 3–4% with the slowest growth (~0.04–0.05 pp per two weeks)**; at those trends the 250+ class would take ~6 years to reach 25% adoption and the 1–4 class ~15 years. — [Census blog](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/blogs/research-matters/2024/12/ai-use-small-businesses.html)
- Later BTOS readings (secondary): small firms (<250) 6.3% (early 2025) → 8.8% (Aug 2025) vs 11.1% large (SBA Office of Advocacy, Sept 2025, as relayed by [Capsule CRM](https://capsulecrm.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-statistics/)); production-based measure 4.6% (early 2024) → 10% (Sept 2025), broader any-function measure 17.3% ([EIG analysis](https://eig.org/?p=24816)). SBA page returned 404 and EIG 404 when fetched; treat the 2025 figures as secondary until pulled from the [BTOS data tool](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/). Caution: BTOS measures AI in *production*, a narrow definition; it says nothing about hours.
- Big I / Future One 2026 Agency Universe Study (Zeldis Research, n=1,376 agencies) — STRONG for its industry: 46% of independent agencies "use AI" (up from 15% in 2024), mostly for marketing content (49%), coverage-form analysis (43%), contract review (35%); top barrier "lack of knowledge about capability" 60%. — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-editorsnote/2026/10/05/887591.htm). Shows "uses AI" ≠ automated back office.

**Staff time**
- Not found. See Gaps.

**BLS American Time Use Survey** — STRONG but not applicable: ATUS codes time by activity (e.g., "work, main job") and does not split paid work time into admin vs. non-admin tasks, nor by firm size. No usable figure; listed so the writer does not go looking.

### Inferences
- Taking the two 2023–2024 US owner surveys at face value and applying them to a 45–60-hour owner week gives roughly 13–20 owner-hours per week on admin; the honest range to state is "roughly a fifth to a third of the owner's week", labelled as vendor-survey evidence.
- The government data point that *is* solid is adoption: in 2024, only ~3–4% of firms in the 5–99 employee range reported using AI in production, the lowest and slowest-growing size classes — which is the best available evidence that in this band the admin work is still done manually.

### Gaps
- No US survey found that measures *staff* (not owner) admin hours in 5–50-person firms. Not found.
- NFIB "Small Business Problems and Priorities" 2024 edition: not located in search (last confirmed edition 2016 ranked "unreasonable government regulations" #2 of 75; 2005 edition: among those troubled by regulation, >25% cited extra paperwork). — [Daily Signal on 2016 edition](https://www.dailysignal.com/2016/10/27/rising-red-tape-near-top-of-small-business-fears/). Needs the NFIB PDF directly.
- Bank of America Business Owner Report, Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses Voices, US Chamber/MetLife Small Business Index, SCORE: no 2022–2026 figure on admin hours found from any of them. The "SCORE: 23 hours/week" line circulating on aggregator sites could not be traced to SCORE and should not be used.
- Census Annual Business Survey 2023 automation-technology module results: the module exists (2019/2023 cycle per a 2024 BEA presentation) but published 2023 tables were not located.

## Key Question 3: What do occupational employment counts and task descriptions say about these roles?

### Takeaway
The six target clerical occupations employ about 7.4 million people nationally (BLS 2025 base-year counts via O*NET), all projected to decline over 2025–2035 yet with ~726,000 openings a year from replacement. Their O*NET core tasks are overwhelmingly data transfer, record-keeping, scheduling and phone handling — the activity types McKinsey and Goldman score highest for automatability.

### Inferences
- Summing the six occupations gives ~7.37 million workers whose core tasks are overwhelmingly "move information from A to B, check it, file it, schedule it" — the task class every exposure study scores highest.
- BLS projects decline in every one of these occupations while still needing ~726,000 hires a year to replace leavers; for a 5–50-person firm this is the practical pain: the role is hard to backfill, and the work does not go away.

### Gaps
- Share of each occupation employed in firms of 5–50 employees: BLS OEWS does not publish by establishment size; the Census Statistics of US Businesses does by industry but not by occupation. Not found.
- OEWS May 2025 payroll counts themselves were not retrievable (bls.gov blocks automated fetching); the O*NET-republished EP counts above are the fallback.

## Key Question 4: Trade-specific studies (surveying, title/escrow, property management, CPA, insurance, law, medical/dental front desk, freight brokers, contractors, HVAC/plumbing)

### Takeaway
Hard, primary-source time measurements exist for only a few of the listed trades: medical practices (AMA: 13 hours/week of physician+staff time per physician on prior authorization, 40 PAs/week, 40% have staff who do nothing else; phone still the top channel), law firms (Clio: lawyers bill ~2.9 of 8 hours; the rest is largely admin), construction (FMI/PlanGrid: 5.5 h/week hunting project data, >14 h/week non-optimal), title (ALTA 2026: >80% of purchase files need 11+ documents reviewed; 21% need >50 records), and insurance agencies (Applied/Ivans 2026: 74% of agents name re-keying into carrier portals as the top pain point). For surveying, freight brokerage, CPA firms, property management and HVAC/plumbing, only anecdotes or vendor marketing were found.

### Cited Findings

**Medical / dental front desk — STRONG (AMA annual survey; MGMA)**
- AMA 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Survey (44-question web survey, December 2025, n=1,000 practicing physicians from Medscape panel, 40% primary care/60% specialists, screened to ≥20 h/week patient care and completing PAs): "On average, practices complete **40** PAs per physician, per week"; "Physicians and their staff spend **13 hours** each week completing PAs"; "**40%** of physicians have staff who work exclusively on PA"; "Physicians report **phone** as the most commonly used method for completing PAs for medical services"; "Only 24% of physicians report that their EHR system offers electronic PA for prescription medications"; 88% say PA interferes with continuity of care. — [AMA survey PDF](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/prior-authorization-survey.pdf)
- Earlier AMA waves for trend: Dec 2016 survey — 37 PAs/week, 16.4 hours/week of physician+staff time, ~one-third had staff working exclusively on PA, fax and phone most common. — [AMA article, Jan 2017](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/survey-quantifies-time-burdens-prior-authorization). Caution: PA is one slice of front-desk paperwork (no intake, insurance verification, recall or billing time measured); physician-reported, so staff hours are estimates; per-physician, so a 3-doctor practice ≈ 39 staff-hours/week on PA alone.
- MGMA Annual Regulatory Burden Report (Nov 2024/2025, 350–400+ group practices): 82–89% rate PA "very or extremely burdensome"; 89–92% have hired or redeployed staff solely for PA; PA staffing spend up 43% 2019–2024 (secondary coverage; year attribution conflicts). — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/practices-say-prior-authorization-and-other-regulatory-burdens-have-increased-last-year); [Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/finance/prior-authorizations-are-top-burden-for-medical-groups-mgma.html); [MGMA Stat Sept 2026: 44% say payer turnaround got slower](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/fewer-than-1-in-10-see-prior-auth-turnarounds-faster). Caution: MGMA members skew to larger groups than a 5–50-person practice; figures are attitudes, not hours.
- Dental-specific: not found.

**Law offices — STRONG for Clio-user population (aggregated anonymised data from tens of thousands of Clio users + surveys), but primary pages returned 403**
- Clio Legal Trends Report 2024 (secondary): lawyers log ~2.9 billable hours per 8-hour day (37% utilization, up from ~29–30% in earlier editions); of non-billable time, ~48% goes to administrative tasks, 33% business development, 19% other. — [Attorney at Work](https://www.attorneyatwork.com/?p=99999830); [ABA Journal on earlier edition: 2.24 h/day](https://abajournal.com/news/article/lawyers_are_only_billing_a_fraction_of_their_time_how_can_they_be_more_effi); [NALFA](https://www.thenalfa.org/blog/report-some-lawyers-spend-30-percent-of-workday-billing-clients/)
- Clio Legal Trends 2025: AI use among legal professionals 79% (vs 19% a year earlier) — Clio's own framing is "in some capacity"; separately, only 8% of solo and 4% of small firms have adopted AI "widely or universally" (2025 Solo & Small Firm report). Mid-size firm report: utilization 34–49% since 2016. — [2Civility summary](https://www.2civility.org/2025-clio-legal-trends-report/); [Clio mid-size PDF](https://www.clio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/202603-Legal-Trends-Report-for-Mid-Size-Law-Firms-Reduced.pdf); [Clio press release](https://www.clio.com/?p=47089). Caution: Clio users skew solo/small — good for this brief — but are self-selected software adopters; "administrative" is Clio's own survey category.

**Construction contractors — STRONG-ish (FMI, n≈600 US construction professionals; commissioned by PlanGrid/Autodesk, a vendor)**
- "Construction Disconnected" (FMI & PlanGrid, Aug 2018): >14 hours per person per week (~35% of working time) on "non-optimal" activities: ~5.5 h/week looking for project data (revised drawings, cut sheets), ~5 h/week resolving conflicts, ~4 h/week assessing rework; poor communication = 26% of rework ($17bn/yr), poor project information = 22% ($14.3bn/yr); total >$177bn/yr US. — [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/industry-could-be-overspending-177b-per-year-study-finds/529450/); [Autodesk blog (403 when fetched)](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/construction-disconnected-fmi-report/). Caution: 2018; respondents are project-side professionals (PMs, supers), not office staff; vendor-commissioned though FMI ran it.
- AGC/ABC: no time-use figures found.

**Title & escrow — STRONG (ALTA, n=449 title professionals, 47 states, published March 2026)**
- "Measuring the Complexity of Title Production: A Study of Operational Demands, Risks, and Curative Challenges": >80% of purchase transactions require reviewing at least 11 documents; 21% involve >50 records tied to ownership history; nearly 60% of transactions require clearing 3–5 title issues before closing; >50% of title professionals spend ≥11 hours/month on fraud prevention. — [HousingWire](https://housingwire.com/articles/alta-study-title-production-complexity-curative-work) (ALTA's own page not reachable; ALTA lists a "Title Insurance Curative Work Study" under [Critical Issue Studies](https://www.alta.org/business-operations/research-initiatives-and-resources/critical-issue-studies)). Caution: counts of documents/issues, not hours per file; ALTA is an advocacy body with an interest in showing complexity.

**Insurance agencies — STRONG for industry surveys (Big I n=1,376; Applied/Ivans n=702), but vendor-run for the re-keying figure**
- Applied Systems / Ivans "2026 Agency-Carrier Connectivity Trends" (702 independent agents, April–May 2026): re-keying risk data into multiple carrier portals is the #1 pain point for 74%; 70% for commercial-heavy vs 62% personal-lines agencies; 90% have reduced business with a carrier over submission friction. — [Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/re-keying-and-workflow-friction-are-costing-carriers-placement-ivans-survey-finds/); [Coverager](https://coverager.com/agents-are-choosing-carriers-that-automate-submissions-say-findings-in-2026-insurance-agency-carrier-connectivity-trends-survey-report/). Caution: Applied sells the fix; measures pain-point ranking, not hours.
- Big I 2026 Agency Universe Study: 37,000 agencies (down from 39,000 in 2024); average 9.9 staff (up from 8.2) — i.e., the typical independent agency sits squarely in the 5–50 band; top technology challenge "dealing with multiple carrier interfaces" (no % given); 46% use AI. — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-editorsnote/2026/10/05/887591.htm)
- The widely quoted "agents spend two hours a day re-keying" (attributed to EZLynx) could not be sourced: WEAK/unsourced; do not use.

### Inferences
- The strongest trade-specific "still manual" evidence is in healthcare (phone is still the #1 PA channel; only 24% have e-PA for drugs in the EHR) and insurance (74% name re-keying across carrier portals as their top problem) — both are literal "retyping between systems" cases.
- For law and construction, the measurable waste is time *not* on the core billable/production task (lawyers bill 2.9 of 8 hours; construction professionals lose 5.5 h/week finding documents).
- For surveying, title, freight, CPA, property management and HVAC, the writer should lean on occupation-level exposure (Q1) and task lists (Q3) rather than claim a trade-specific hours figure.

### Gaps
- Surveying (NSPS), freight brokerage (TIA/DAT), CPA firms (AICPA/CPA.com), property management (NARPM), HVAC/plumbing (US), dental front desk: no primary time-use measurement found for 2022–2026. Not found.
- Clio's primary 2024/2025 report pages and Brookings' report page could not be fetched (403); figures above are from their own press/summary coverage and should be re-checked against the PDFs before quoting precisely (especially the 2.9 h/day and "48% of non-billable time is admin").
- MGMA regulatory-burden percentages conflict across outlets (82% vs 89% "very/extremely burdensome"; 89% vs 92% hired/redeployed staff); the MGMA PDF was not reached.
- ALTA's own report page for the 2026 complexity study was not located; only HousingWire's write-up.
