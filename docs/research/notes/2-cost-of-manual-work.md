# What manual office/admin work costs US small-business owners (2022–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-28. Grading: **STRONG** = government, academic, or large-sample with published method; **WEAK** = vendor survey, small sample, secondary aggregator, or marketing. Every row carries a one-line caution on how far it can be stretched.

## Wages: what the people who do busywork are paid (BLS OEWS)

### Takeaway
As of May 2025 (the latest OEWS, released May 15, 2026), the national median for the core "busywork" occupations runs from $18.27/hr (receptionists) to $24.36/hr (bookkeeping clerks), with office supervisors at $33.41/hr. Florida medians sit 3–8% below national. May 2024 figures were not retrievable through the BLS API (it only returns the current year); they live in the May 2024 archived tables and are not reproduced here.

### Inferences
- A "blended busywork hour" for a small office (admin assistant / office clerk / bookkeeper mix) is roughly **$22–$24/hr median base wage** nationally and **$21–$24/hr in Florida** before any employer overhead.
- Owner-level time priced at the general-manager median is about **$50/hr nationally, $49/hr in Florida** before overhead — double the clerk rate.

### Gaps
- May 2024 OEWS values: not captured. The BLS API returns only the latest reference year for OEWS; the May 2024 values are in the archived national XLSX (`oesm24nat.zip`) linked from https://www.bls.gov/oes/tables.htm. The 2024→2025 change is therefore not computed here.
- Percentile wages (10th/25th/75th/90th) were not retrieved; they are on each occupation's profile page and in the One-Screen tool.

## Loaded cost: benefits, payroll overhead, recruiting, training

### Takeaway
BLS puts benefits at **31.3% of total compensation** for private-industry office and administrative support workers (June 2026), but only **25.9%** at establishments with 1–49 workers — so a small firm's realistic "loaded" multiplier on wages is about **1.35× (1/0.741)**, not the 1.4–1.5× often quoted. Recruiting adds a one-off ~$4,700–$5,500 average (SHRM; medians are far lower) and ~39 days to fill.

### Cited Findings
- **ECEC, June 2026, private industry, all workers:** total compensation $46.89/hr; wages and salaries $32.82 (70.0%); benefits $14.07 (30.0%) — [BLS ECEC news release, Table 1/4](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm). Released September 9, 2026. **STRONG.**
- **ECEC Table 4, June 2026, Office and administrative support occupations (private industry):** total compensation $36.73/hr; wages $25.22 (68.7%); total benefits $11.51 (31.3%); paid leave 7.5%; supplemental pay 2.9%; insurance 11.0%; retirement 2.9%; legally required 7.0% — [BLS ECEC Table 4](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.t04.htm). **STRONG.** Caution: blends all establishment sizes; large employers pull the insurance and retirement shares up.
- **ECEC Table 4, Sales and office occupations:** total $36.34; wages 71.5%; benefits 28.5% — same table. **STRONG.**
- **ECEC Table 6, June 2026, private industry by establishment size:** 1–49 workers: total $37.64/hr, wages $27.88 (74.1%), benefits $9.76 (25.9%), legally required benefits $0.87 (2.3%)*; 1–99 workers: total $38.43, wages 73.7%, benefits 26.3%; 50–99: benefits 27.3%; 100–499: 30.3%; 500+: 35.3% — [BLS ECEC Table 6](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.t06.htm). **STRONG.** Caution: this is all occupations at small establishments, not admin occupations at small establishments (BLS does not cross-tab size × occupation in the release). *The "legally required 2.3%" line as extracted looks implausibly low versus the 7.0–7.2% in Table 4 and should be re-read from the table before use; the wage/benefit split is the usable figure.
- **Legally required benefits (FICA, UI, workers' comp), private full-time workers:** $3.76/hr, 7.0% of compensation — [BLS ECEC release, Table 5](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm). **STRONG.**
- **SHRM average cost-per-hire "nearly $4,700":** SHRM benchmarking data reported in an April 11, 2022 SHRM article; the same article quotes consultant Edie Goldberg that "many employers estimate the total cost to hire a new employee can be three to four times the position's salary" — [SHRM, "The Real Costs of Recruitment"](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/real-costs-recruitment). **WEAK-to-moderate** (SHRM member survey; the 3–4× salary line is one consultant's opinion, not a measurement). Caution: average is skewed by large employers and executive hires; SHRM's own Talent Access figures were reported elsewhere as average $4,683 / median $1,244.
- **SHRM 2025 Benchmarking:** nonexecutive average cost-per-hire $5,475, executive $35,879; fielded Jan 9–Mar 3, 2025 from a random sample of SHRM members; SHRM's 2025 recruiting data brief separately reports **medians** of $1,200 (nonexecutive) and $10,625 (executive) — [SHRM 2025 Recruiting Benchmarking Report (PDF)](https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/research/2025-recruiting-benchmarking-report.pdf); [SHRM 2025 recruiting benchmarking page](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-recruiting-benchmarking). **Moderate** (large sample, but SHRM members skew to firms with HR departments). Caution: for a 10-person firm the median ($1,200) is the more honest number.
- **SHRM 2026 Benchmarking (over 4,600 organizations):** nonexecutive median time-to-fill **39 calendar days**, down from the prior year; 97% of nonexecutive positions filled externally — [SHRM recruiting benchmarking page](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-recruiting-benchmarking). **Moderate.**
- **Training Industry Report 2024 (Training magazine):** average 47 hours of training per employee (down from 57 in 2023); average $774 per learner; "small" companies (100–999 employees) $1,047 per learner — figures as summarized by [Devlin Peck's training statistics round-up](https://www.devlinpeck.com/content/employee-training-statistics?form=MG0AV3) and [learnexperts.ai](https://learnexperts.ai/?p=5058). **WEAK** (secondary summaries of a magazine survey; "small" starts at 100 employees, so nothing covers <100). Caution: do not present as a sub-100-employee figure.
- **Intuit/Kelton payroll survey (1,006 US employers, published Jan 31, 2020):** owners spend "nearly five hours each pay period" on payroll (≈111 min calculating payroll taxes, 96 min filing, 86 min allocating funding); 63% "never realized how much time they spent on payroll taxes"; pay frequency: biweekly 42%, weekly 38%, monthly 15% — [Bloomberg Tax summary](https://news.bloombergtax.com/payroll/survey-small-business-owners-misjudge-time-to-prep-payroll). **WEAK** (vendor-commissioned, pre-2022). Caution: dated; use only as "order of magnitude ≈ 10 hrs/month at biweekly pay."

### Inferences
- **Loaded hourly cost worked example (small firm):** admin assistant median $22.86/hr ÷ 0.741 (1–49-worker wage share) ≈ **$30.85/hr loaded**; bookkeeping clerk $24.36 ÷ 0.741 ≈ **$32.90/hr**; office clerk $21.64 ÷ 0.741 ≈ **$29.20/hr**. Using the occupation-specific 68.7% wage share instead gives $33.30 / $35.50 / $31.50. State the assumption whichever you use.
- Annualized: a full-time admin assistant at the national median costs roughly **$64,000–$69,000/yr loaded** (2,080 hrs × $30.85–$33.30), plus a one-off $1,200–$5,500 to recruit and ~39 days of vacancy.
- "30 hours a month" of clerk-grade busywork ≈ **$900–$1,000/month loaded** at median wages; the same 30 hours of owner time at the GM median ≈ **$1,500+/month** before overhead.

### Gaps
- No BLS cross-tab exists for benefits share of *admin occupations at small establishments*; the 25.9% (size) and 31.3% (occupation) figures bracket it.
- SHRM full 2025/2026 reports are member-gated; cost-per-hire by organization size was not visible on the public pages.
- No credible 2022–2026 source found for *training time to proficiency* for admin/clerical roles specifically.

## Hours and overtime: how much time goes to admin, invoicing, chasing payments, data entry, scheduling

### Takeaway
Every recent US estimate is a self-reported survey, mostly vendor-commissioned: owners report roughly **a fifth to a third of their week** on admin — UENI (2024, n=837 microbusinesses) 22.4% of a 60-hour median week; Time etc/Censuswide (2023, n=251) 36% of a 45.5-hour week (≈16 hrs). No government time-diary data isolates small-firm admin hours, and no US source measured overtime caused by admin work.

### Cited Findings
- **UENI (May 6–13, 2024; 837 US businesses with ≤5 employees):** median workweek **60 hours**; 62% work >50 hrs/week; administration + legal + compliance = **22.4%** of owner time, "79% more than the amount of time spent on marketing and sales"; admin defined as managing staff, finances, scheduling appointments and inventory — [UENI, "How microbusiness owners spend their time"](https://ueni.com/en-us/small-business-research/how-microbusiness-owners-spend-their-time). **WEAK** (website-builder vendor; convenience sample; includes legal/compliance). Caution: 22.4% × 60 hrs ≈ 13.4 hrs/week is a derived figure, not reported; sample is micro (≤5 staff), so don't apply to 20–50-person firms.
- **Time etc / Censuswide (Sept 22–28, 2023; 251 US entrepreneurs, growth-mode, trading ≥2 years, no freelancers/sole traders):** average week 45.5 hrs; 29% work >50 hrs; **36% of the week on admin on average**; 31% spend 26–50% of their week on small admin tasks; 99% do some admin; tasks done in a typical week: log expenses 59%, research 49%, schedule management 45%, **create invoices 44%, data entry 43%**, order supplies 40%, format documents 29%, **chase late payers 27%** — [Time etc, "The big price of small tasks"](https://timeetc.com/resources/how-to-achieve-more/the-big-price-of-small-tasks-how-entrepreneurs-may-be-unwittingly-keeping-their-businesses-small). **WEAK** (virtual-assistant vendor, n=251). Caution: 36% × 45.5 ≈ 16.4 hrs/week is derived; vendor sells delegation.
- **Sage / Plum Consulting, "Sweating the Small Stuff" (Sept 2017; >3,000 business builders, 11 countries):** small businesses spend ~**120 working days a year** on admin (~5% of total manpower); accounting is the heaviest admin task (nearly a quarter of all admin for US SMBs), followed by invoicing and taxation; some coverage of the same report cites 230–240 days (17%) for the smallest firms, unreconciled — [CPA Practice Advisor summary](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/small-business/news/12367641/smbs-spend-120-working-days-per-year-on-admin-tasks-and-bookkeeping); [Sage press release (GlobeNewswire)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2017/09/25/1158001/0/en/Sage-survey-shows-that-unleashing-business-builders-from-burdensome-admin-could-unlock-over-C-728bn-in-lost-productivity.html). **WEAK** and **2017** (label as such). Caution: multi-country, firm-level not owner-level, vendor.
- **Intuit QuickBooks (Oct 2022):** each US small business "saves roughly 73 lost working days" per year when it has an accountant or bookkeeper — reported in [Intuit's datawrapper chart](https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/9EeKf/1/) and the [Xero time-tax guide](https://www.xero.com/us/guides/time-tax-small-business/) search context. **WEAK** (vendor; self-reported savings). Caution: this is claimed time saved, not time spent.
- **NFIB Small Business Problems & Priorities 2024 (11th ed.; 2,873 usable questionnaires from 40,000 mailed to NFIB members, Feb–mid-Apr 2024; 75 problems rated 1 "critical" to 7 "not a problem"):** Table 1 ranks: #1 Cost of Health Insurance (mean 2.58; 40.5% "critical"); #2 Cost of Supplies/Inventories (19.9% critical); #5 Locating Qualified Employees (28.0%); #7 Unreasonable Government Regulations (23.9%); #14 Finding and Keeping Skilled Employees (24.2%); **#18 Federal Paperwork (mean 3.73; 13.2% critical; was #15 in 2020)**; **#19 State/Local Paperwork (3.73; 12.5%; was #11)**; **#37 Cash Flow (4.19; 10.2%; was #26)**; #43 Managing Employees (9.5%); #44 Training Employees (7.2%; was #32); **#47 Using Computer(s), the Internet or New Technology Effectively (4.43; 6.7%)** — [NFIB 2024 Small Business Problems & Priorities (PDF)](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024-Small-Business-Problems-Priorities.pdf), Table 1 (approx. pp. 7–9) and the "Employee Size of Business" breakouts (p. 39 ff.). **STRONG** for what it measures (large sample, published method) but note it is NFIB members (skews older, established firms) and measures *perceived severity*, not hours. Caution: paperwork and cash flow rank mid-pack; don't claim owners rank admin as a top-5 problem.
- **Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey, 2024 survey year (fielded Sept–Nov 2024; 7,653 employer firms, 1–499 employees; convenience sample):** financial challenges in prior 12 months — rising costs of goods/services/wages 75%; paying operating expenses 56%; **uneven cash flows 51%** (SBCS notes "uneven cash flow" *includes collecting on receivables*); operational: reaching customers/growing sales 57% (up from 53%) — [Fed SBCS 2025 Report on Employer Firms](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2025/2025-report-on-employer-firms). **STRONG** (large sample; but not random). Caution: "uneven cash flow" is broader than late invoices.

- **Cornerstone Advisors (Q4 2023; reported as 750 US small-business owners/executives):** 20.5 hours/week of accounting work across bookkeeping, invoicing (4.1 hrs), expense tracking (4.2), financial reporting (4.2) and income taxes — only found via the secondary page [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-finance-administration-workload-statistics-2026). **WEAK / UNVERIFIED** (primary report not located). Caution: do not cite without finding the Cornerstone original.
- **Melio-cited 2021 survey:** "65% of businesses spend about 14 hours a week on administrative tasks related to collecting payments" — [Contrary Research on Melio](https://research.contrary.com/report/melio). **WEAK** (secondary, 2021, vendor). 
- **EBizCharge (820 finance leaders, Q4 2025):** nearly 1 in 3 companies have teams spending >30 hours/month on manual payment tasks — [EBizCharge blog](https://ebizcharge.com/blog/the-state-of-payment-automation-in-2026-why-1-in-3-businesses-still-waste-a-work-week-each-month-on-payment-tasks/). **WEAK** (vendor; not small-business specific).
- **Overtime (BLS):** manufacturing production workers averaged 3.8 overtime hours/week in 2025 (3.6 in 2023–24); BLS publishes overtime hours only for manufacturing — [BLS TED, overtime hours in manufacturing](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/overtime-hours-in-manufacturing-industries.htm). **STRONG** but irrelevant to office work.

### Inferences
- Converging on **13–16 hours/week of owner admin** across the two 2023–24 US surveys, despite different definitions and samples — a defensible range if labeled as "owner-reported."
- Invoicing and data entry are each done weekly by **~43–44% of owners** (Time etc), which is the most specific task-level evidence found for the "quotes and invoices" story.

### Gaps
- No US government (BLS ATUS/CPS) measure of admin hours by firm size exists; ATUS does not code "business admin" separately for self-employed owners.
- No source quantified **overtime hours caused by admin** in small firms; "overtime" evidence is absent.
- "SCORE 2025 Time Allocation Survey (23 hrs/week admin)" appears only on an aggregator and could not be verified against SCORE; treat as unsourced.
- Xero, Starling, Bill.com, Melio: no 2022–2026 US hours surveys with stated methodology were found; UK figures (Starling, Sage UK "11 hours/week on admin/finance", Dext Australia 8–12 hrs) exist but are non-US.

## Errors and rework: manual data-entry error rates and the cost of invoicing errors / late invoices

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed data-entry studies put single-key manual entry at roughly **0.4–7 errors per 1,000 fields** depending on field type, with double entry cutting that by 50–90%. Late payment is the better-documented cost: **59% of US small businesses had invoices 30+ days overdue in 2026 (Intuit), averaging $17.7K owed**, and Atradius found **half of US B2B invoices overdue, 8% of credit sales written off** (2024).

### Cited Findings
- **Paulsen, Overgaard & Lauritsen, PLoS ONE 7(4):e35087 (2012); 398 questionnaires, 21,887 data fields:** single-key entry **0.370 errors per 1,000 fields** (95% CI 0.160–0.729) on optical-mark forms; double-key entry 0.046/1,000; for handwritten (ICR) fields single-key 6.734/1,000 and double-key 3.367/1,000; the paper cites Jinks et al. (2003) at 0.041% (≈0.41/1,000) and earlier work finding automated processing took one-half to one-third the time of manual entry — [PMC3320865](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3320865/). **STRONG** (academic) but a clinical-questionnaire setting. Caution: tick-box forms are far easier than free-text invoices; the ICR (handwriting) rate, ~0.7%, is the closer analogue to retyping paper into a system.
- **Barchard & Pace (2011) and Kawado et al. (2003), as summarized in a UNLV conference handout:** double entry produced significantly fewer errors than single entry and visual checking; visual checking produced 2,958% more errors than double entry; double entry caught 88.3% of errors vs 59.5% for partner read-aloud — [UNLV handout (PDF)](https://img.faculty.unlv.edu/lab/conference-presentations/Conference%20Posters%205/To%20Err%20is%20Human%20Tina%20Misha%20Handout%2010%20April%208%202015.pdf). **Moderate** (academic, secondary summary, pre-2022).
- **Intuit QuickBooks Small Business Late Payments Report 2026 (US; Intuit Small Business Insights quarterly panel ≈5,000, plus a Dec 2025 online survey of 1,305 US owners with 0–250 employees):** **59% have invoices overdue 30+ days, up from 47% a year earlier**; average **$17.7K owed** among businesses with unpaid invoices (vs $17.5K prior year); 22% have ≥20% of invoices unpaid past 30 days; 39% say one late payment made it hard to cover payroll or bills in the past year; 27% say a missed payment under $5,000 caused that strain; in 2025, 26% delayed their own salary, 19% took on debt/credit-card balances they otherwise wouldn't, 18% paid a bill late and incurred a fee; 51% of businesses with overdue invoices report cash-flow problems vs 36% without — [QuickBooks late payments report 2026](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2026/). **WEAK-to-moderate** (vendor, but large panel and stated method). Caution: averages are among firms *with* unpaid invoices; Intuit customers skew to invoicing businesses.
- **Intuit QuickBooks 2025 edition (Jan 2025; 2,487 US small businesses):** 56% owed money on unpaid invoices, averaging $17.5K; 47% had invoices >30 days overdue — [QuickBooks late payments report 2025](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2025/). **WEAK-to-moderate.**
- **Atradius Payment Practices Barometer, United States 2024 (fielded end-Q2/early-Q3 2024):** "half of B2B invoices are currently overdue"; bad debts average **8% of B2B credit sales**; overdue invoices "turned into cash on average 20 days past due"; payment terms averaged 30 days — [Atradius US 2024 report](https://atradius.us/reports/payment-practices-barometer-b2b-payment-practices-trends-united-states-2024.html). **WEAK-to-moderate** (credit insurer survey; sample size not stated on page; 2025 US edition reportedly n=240 with 43% of invoice value overdue and 5% written off, per a secondary blog, unverified). Caution: Atradius samples B2B firms of all sizes, skewing larger than Main Street.
- **Atradius North America, Sept 2026:** overdue invoices average 23% of B2B receivables; 43% of sales on credit terms — per the search summary of the [Atradius 2026 North America release](https://atradius.us/knowledge-and-research/reports/b2b-payment-practices-trends-united-states-2024) (the 2024 US page links to the 2026 report). **WEAK** (not independently fetched).
- **Fed SBCS:** "uneven cash flow" (which *includes collecting on receivables*) was a financial challenge for **51%** of employer firms in the 2024 survey year — [Fed SBCS 2025 report](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2025/2025-report-on-employer-firms); definition from the [2026 Florida chartbook notes](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-firms-in-focus-chartbooks/sbcs_chartbook2026_florida.pdf). **STRONG.**
- **MGMA Stat poll, March 5, 2024 (235 applicable responses):** **60% of medical group leaders reported higher claim denial rates in 2024 vs the same period of 2023**; 29% about the same; 11% decreased — [MGMA Stat](https://mgma.com/mgma-stat/strategic-improvements-in-your-rcm-to-reduce-your-practices-claim-denials). **WEAK-to-moderate** (small, self-selected poll of a trade body). A January 2026 MGMA Stat poll (288 responses) found 48% named denials/appeals the biggest revenue-cycle leak — [Physicians Practice](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/mgma-poll-denials-are-biggest-revenue-cycle-leak-for-medical-practices). **WEAK-to-moderate.**
- **MGMA-attributed rework cost ≈ $25 per denied claim and ~11% median denial rate** circulate only through vendor blogs (e.g., [Rivet Health](https://www.rivethealth.com/blog/denials-revenue-cycle-management), [Harine Management](https://harinemanagement.com/blog/good-denial-rate-medical-practice)); the AAFP (2015) cited MGMA that >50% of denied claims are never reworked — [AAFP FPM](https://www.aafp.org/fpm/2015/0300/p7.html). **WEAK** (secondary; primary MGMA data not located). Caution: healthcare-specific; use only as a proxy for "error → rework cost" with that label.
- **Cost per invoice processed (AP):** Ardent Partners 2025 — all-buyer average **$10.89 and 10.9 days per invoice**; top performers $2.78 and 3.1 days; exception rates 22% average vs 9% top — as reported by [Corpay's AP benchmarks blog](https://www.corpay.com/en-AU/resources/blog/ap-team-productivity-benchmarks). **WEAK** (vendor citing a vendor; mid-market/enterprise sample). Caution: not small-business data.

### Inferences
- A firm retyping ~2,000 fields a month from paper/handwriting (ICR-like) at 0.3–0.7% would expect **6–14 wrong fields a month**; the cost is the rework and the downstream late/incorrect invoice, which no source prices for small firms directly.
- The late-payment chain is the most defensible dollar story: 59% carry 30+-day-overdue invoices, average $17.7K outstanding, and 39% say a single late payment strained payroll — all Intuit, so present as "an Intuit survey found."

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of error rates for **invoice or quote** data entry in small firms; healthcare questionnaire and clinical-trial studies are the only measured rates.
- No source quantifies the **dollar cost of an invoicing error** (re-issue, dispute, delay) for small businesses; the 1-10-100 "rule" (Labovitz & Chang, 1992) was not verified and is omitted.
- Primary MGMA denial-rate and rework-cost reports (2024–2025) were not reachable; only polls and secondary citations.

## Slow quotes = lost revenue: response time vs win rate

### Takeaway
The only rigorous evidence remains the 2011 HBR audit (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington) — responding within an hour made firms ~7× likelier to qualify a lead — and its "5 minutes vs 30 minutes = 21×" cousin is a 2007 InsideSales.com vendor study. Every 2022–2026 home-services figure (ServiceTitan "28% at 42 minutes," "2-minute responders convert 62%/41%," CallRail 14% missed calls) is vendor-published, internally inconsistent across retellings, and should be labelled WEAK.

### Cited Findings
- **Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," Harvard Business Review, March 2011 (Vol. 89, p. 28):** article record — [BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711); [HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads). The HBR page itself only states that "most companies are not responding nearly fast enough." The figures commonly attributed to it — audit of **2,241 US companies**, average response time **42 hours**, **23% never responded**, firms responding within an hour were **nearly 7× as likely to qualify the lead** as those responding an hour later (and >60× vs 24 hours+) — appear only in secondary retellings, e.g. [Xictron (vendor blog)](https://www.xictron.com/en/blog/b2b-request-for-quote-rfq-workflow-shop-2026/). **STRONG** study, **2011**, figures **not verified from the full text here**. Caution: it measures *qualifying* a lead, not winning the job, and the firms were mostly larger B2B/B2C companies with web forms.
- **"5 minutes vs 30 minutes: 21× more likely to qualify; 100× more likely to make contact":** 2007 Lead Response Management study by Dr. James Oldroyd, sponsored by InsideSales.com; described as vendor data, not peer-reviewed — [PipelineOn's review of the study](https://pipelineon.com/blog/lead-response-time-study/). **WEAK** (vendor, 2007). Caution: frequently mis-dated to 2023–2025 in marketing copy.
- **ServiceTitan (attributed to its 2025 Home Services Benchmark Report):** average home-service company converts **28% of inbound leads** at a **42-minute** average response; companies responding within 2 minutes convert 62% — per [PipelineOn](https://pipelineon.com/blog/responding-to-leads-faster-home-service/). A different vendor retelling says ServiceTitan's analysis of 28,000 leads found a **41%** booking rate for 2-minute responders, 5× the after-1-hour rate — [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/home-service-lead-response-automation). **WEAK** (primary report not located; 62% vs 41% conflict). ServiceTitan's own page on speed-to-lead: [ServiceTitan blog](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/speed-to-lead-ai-virtual-agent).
- **ServiceTitan 2026 Residential Survey (as reported):** only 50% of contractors respond to new leads within the first hour — [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/home-service-lead-response-automation-workflow-guide-2026). **WEAK.**
- **CallRail small-business benchmark (Jan 14, 2025 release):** missed-call rate by industry — healthcare 32%, legal 28%, **home services 14%**, real estate 9%; "up to 85% of customers whose calls go unanswered will not call back" — [BusinessWire release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114793850/en/CallRail-Releases-Report-Benchmarking-Marketing-Efforts-for-Small-Businesses) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; figures from search summary). **WEAK** (vendor platform data; the 85% claim is unsourced). 
- **Invoca Home Services Lead Conversion Benchmarks (July 2026):** a person answered only **52%** of inbound home-services calls (65% after excluding calls ≤15 seconds) — [Invoca report page](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026). **WEAK** (vendor platform data; definition differs from CallRail's).
- **Rotabull (aviation parts marketplace, 100,000 quotes):** buyers are most likely to receive a quote within the first hour of sending an RFQ; response probability drops steeply after one hour and plateaus around six hours — [Rotabull, "When will I get a quote" (2023)](https://rotabull.com/blog/when-will-i-get-a-quote-2023). **WEAK** (observational platform data; niche B2B).
- **Housecall Pro 2025 homeowner survey (>1,000 US homeowners):** takeaways centre on service experience, speed and instant estimates; no quote-timing win rate published — [Housecall Pro report](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/). **WEAK.**
- **Jobber Home Service Economic Report (Q2 2026, data from >400,000 users):** tracks New Work Scheduled and Median Revenue; no quote-turnaround or win-rate statistic found in the 2024–2026 editions — [Jobber HSER Aug 2026](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-reports/august-2026/). **WEAK** for this question (nothing relevant).

### Inferences
- Safe phrasing: "A Harvard Business Review audit (2011) found firms that replied within an hour were about seven times likelier to qualify a lead; contractor-software vendors report that most home-service firms take 40+ minutes and miss roughly one call in seven." Avoid quoting any specific 2024–2026 win-rate multiplier as fact.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 **academic** study on quote turnaround and win probability for contractors/service firms was found.
- ServiceTitan's and Jobber's primary benchmark PDFs were not retrieved; the "78% buy from the first responder" claim has no traceable source and is omitted.
- The HBR full text (paywalled) was not read; figures above are as widely quoted.

## Opportunity cost: pricing an owner's hour and revenue per employee in small firms

### Takeaway
Government data give two usable anchors: the OEWS median for general and operations managers (**$50.85/hr nationally, $48.84 Florida, May 2025**) as a proxy for an owner-operator's market wage, and Census SUSB 2022 receipts-by-firm-size for revenue per employee — the latter was *not* retrieved (the Census API and SUSB pages returned no usable rows), so revenue-per-employee must be computed from the SUSB "Data by Enterprise Receipts Size" spreadsheet.

### Cited Findings
- **BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-1021 General and Operations Managers:** US employment 3,503,020; median $50.85/hr ($105,770/yr); mean $64.87/hr ($134,940/yr); Florida median $48.84/hr ($101,580/yr), mean $126,940 — [BLS OEWS](https://data.bls.gov/oes/) (API pull 2026-10-28). **STRONG.** Caution: this is the pay of *employed* managers across all firm sizes; owners of 5–30 person firms often draw less, but it is the standard "replacement wage" proxy.
- **Census SUSB 2022:** annual tables are tabulated by enterprise employment size and, separately, by enterprise receipts size; receipts are published only for Economic Census years (2022 qualifies) and only at the US level; rows are suppressed below three firms — [Census SUSB 2022 annual tables](https://census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html); [SUSB datasets](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb.html). **STRONG** source; figures not extracted.
- **Census Annual Business Survey (2023 ABS = reference year 2022):** ~5.9 million US employer firms; tables AB2200CSA04 (employment size of firm) and AB2200CSA03 (receipts size) include receipts, employment and payroll — [Census ABS Company Summary 2022](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/abs/2022-abs-company-summary.html); [Census press release, Dec 2024](https://census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/employer-businesses.html). **STRONG** source; the API query returned HTTP 302 (no data) during this session.
- **SBA Office of Advocacy (May 2024 issue brief):** small firms' share of receipts fell from 55.7% (1963) to 35.6% (2017); firms with <100 employees saw the largest declines — [SBA Advocacy issue brief (PDF)](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Issue-Brief-Declining-Receipts-Share-of-Small-Firms-2024.pdf). **STRONG** (uses 2017 SUSB). Caution: share data, not per-firm revenue.
- **Intuit "Business Ownership in 2026" (Dec 2025; 1,305 US owners, 0–250 employees):** 59% paid for instant transfer/fast deposit in 2025; 26% delayed their own salary because of processing delays — [QuickBooks 2026 late payments report](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2026/). **WEAK-to-moderate.** (Relevant because owners forgo pay when cash is late.)

### Inferences
- **Pricing "30 hours a month" of owner busywork:** 30 × $50.85 = **$1,526/month ($18,300/yr)** at the national GM median wage; 30 × $48.84 = **$1,465/month** in Florida; using the GM *mean* ($64.87) gives $1,946/month. If the owner instead delegates to a median admin assistant at ~$31/hr loaded, the same 30 hours cost ~$925/month — the gap (~$600/month) is the floor on the owner's opportunity cost, before any revenue the owner could have generated in those hours.
- A "30 hours a month" headline needs a stated basis: it sits inside the 13–16 hrs/week owner-admin range from the UENI and Time etc surveys (≈55–70 hrs/month), so it is conservative relative to the surveys, which is a point in its favour.

### Gaps
- **Revenue per employee / receipts per firm by size class (SUSB 2022):** not obtained. Next step: download `us_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx` or the receipts-size table from the SUSB 2022 datasets page and divide receipts by employment for the <20 and 20–99 enterprise size classes.
- NFIB publishes no owner-hourly-value estimate; none found at SCORE either.
- No academic estimate of small-owner shadow wage for 2022–2026 was found.
