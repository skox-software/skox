# Measured productivity and cost results from automating administrative and document work (2022–2026)

Rating key used throughout: **STRONG** = peer-reviewed, government/official statistics, or large-sample randomized/field study with a control group. **WEAK** = vendor case study, sponsor-hosted benchmark, self-reported survey, press/secondary summary, or no methodology. Where a number was only confirmed through a secondary source, that is said explicitly. Every entry carries: figure, who measured it, year, population, URL, and a one-line stretch caution.

## Key question 1: Generative-AI field experiments on knowledge/office work (writing, support, consulting, coding, legal drafting, medical documentation)

### Takeaway
The credible experiments cluster around 10–40% less time on a well-bounded writing/drafting/support task, with the largest gains for novices and the smallest (sometimes zero or negative) for experts; the one firm-wide 6-month RCT (66 firms, 7,137 workers) found the only robust effect was about 1.4–2 fewer hours/week on email, with no measurable change in meetings or document output. Measured ambient-scribe savings in medicine are real but modest (about 16 minutes of documentation per day in the largest controlled study), far below vendor and press claims.

### Cited Findings

**Customer support (STRONG — NBER WP / published QJE 2025)**
- Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work": staggered rollout of an LLM-based assistant to 5,179 customer-support agents (Fortune 500 software firm, offshore agents). Productivity (issues resolved per hour) up 14% on average; 34% for novice/low-skilled agents; "minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers"; customer sentiment improved; retention improved. Working-paper version issued Apr 2023, revised Nov 2023 — [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161). The published QJE version (vol. 140(2), 2025, pp. 889–942, sample 5,172 agents) reports 15% average and notes the most experienced workers see small speed gains with small quality declines — [SSRN 4426942](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4426942). *Caution:* one firm, chat-based support with a tool tuned on that firm's own best agents; not a general "office" effect, and experts gained little.

**Writing/drafting tasks (STRONG — Science 2023, preregistered experiment)**
- Noy & Zhang, "Experimental Evidence on the Productivity Effects of Generative AI" (Science, 2023): 453 college-educated professionals (marketers, grant writers, consultants, data analysts, HR, managers) doing realistic 20–30 minute writing tasks online; random 50% given ChatGPT. Time on the second task fell by 10 minutes (37%) versus a control mean of 27 minutes (p=0.000); evaluator grades rose 0.45 SD; inequality between workers halved (first-to-second-task grade correlation 0.49 in control vs 0.25 in treatment); 68% of treated participants submitted ChatGPT's output without editing. Attrition 5% control / 10% treatment; 10–20% of controls also used ChatGPT, so estimates are lower bounds — [MIT working-paper PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1.pdf); journal record [Science 10.1126/science.adh2586](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586). The "40% less time / 18% higher quality" figures widely quoted come from the earlier working-paper draft — [secondary summary via NBER search](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/w33795.pdf). *Caution:* short, self-contained, incentivized online tasks with no firm context, no downstream review, and self-reported time; this is an upper bound for "drafting a memo", not for end-to-end admin work.

**Management consulting (STRONG design; working paper, not yet peer-reviewed — figures confirmed only via secondary summary this session)**
- Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (HBS Working Paper 24-013 / SSRN 4573321, Sept 2023): preregistered experiment with 758 BCG consultants (~7% of its workforce) randomized to no AI, GPT-4, or GPT-4 plus prompt training. On tasks inside AI's capability frontier (18 creative/analytical/writing tasks), AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with quality rated ~40% higher; below-average performers gained ~43% and top performers ~17%. On a task designed to sit outside the frontier, AI users were less likely to reach the correct answer — [SSRN 4573321](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321); figures confirmed via [8020 newsletter summary](https://the8020newsletter.substack.com/p/consulting-generative-ai) (the HBS and SSRN PDFs returned 403 during this research). *Caution:* elite consultants on designed tasks; the "jagged frontier" result means quality can fall on tasks that look similar but exceed the model.

**Software developers — lab RCT (STRONG design, small sample)**
- Peng, Kalliamvakou, Cihon & Demirer (Microsoft/GitHub/MIT, Feb 2023): controlled experiment, developers recruited on Upwork asked to build an HTTP server in JavaScript; treated group with GitHub Copilot finished 55.8% faster (95% CI 21–89%); less-experienced and older developers benefited most. Sample of 95 is reported by secondary write-ups only — [arXiv 2302.06590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590v1); [getdx summary](https://getdx.com/research/impact-of-ai-on-developer-productivity-evidence-from-copilot/). *Caution:* one toy task, wide CI, Copilot-funded.

**Software developers — three enterprise RCTs (STRONG; Management Science 2025)**
- Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng & Salz, "The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work": RCTs at Microsoft, Accenture and an anonymous Fortune 100 manufacturer, 4,867 developers, 2–8 months. IV estimate: usage of Copilot caused a 26.08% (SE 10.3%) increase in weekly completed tasks (pull requests); commits +13.55% (SE 10.0%); builds +38.38% (SE 12.55%); less-experienced developers adopted more and gained more; "each experiment is noisy" — [MIT draft PDF, Feb 2025](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/draft_copilot_experiments.pdf); published as [Management Science 10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535). *Caution:* large standard errors; measures output count not quality; Microsoft co-authors.

**Software developers — counter-evidence (STRONG design, tiny sample)**
- METR, "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity" (July 2025): RCT at the issue level, 16 experienced maintainers, 246 real issues (~2 hours each), Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet. Developers took 19% longer with AI allowed; they had forecast a 24% speed-up and afterwards still believed they had been 20% faster. Authors explicitly do not claim this generalizes to most developers; the page now points to a Feb 2026 follow-up on late-2025 tools — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/). *Caution:* n=16, expert users on codebases they know intimately; the main lesson is that perceived savings can exceed measured savings.

**Firm-wide office deployment — the key general-office RCT (STRONG; NBER WP 33795, rev. Nov 2025)**
- Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica & Stanton, "Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI": field experiment across 66 large firms and 7,137 knowledge workers (Sept 2023–Oct 2024), each firm recruiting 100+ workers randomized to Microsoft 365 Copilot or control, outcomes from telemetry not self-report. Pre-period mean 11.7 hours/week on email. Intent-to-treat: 1.4 fewer hours/week in email (12%); LATE for the 80% who used it: 2 fewer hours (17%); Outlook sessions per week down 11% (17%); about 1.5 hours/week (LATE 2.2) of new 30-minute-plus email-free blocks; out-of-hours work down a quarter hour/week (9%). No detectable change in Teams meeting time (ITT CI −0.01 to +0.21 hours on a 5.22-hour mean), meetings attended, or documents completed (can rule out increases above 11%); documents may have been finished ~2 days (17%) faster but not significant; Word time +0.14 hours (not significant). Average usage only ~33% of weeks in months 4–6 and varied widely by firm — [NBER w33795](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795). *Caution:* the cleanest estimate of what a generic copilot does for a whole office; it moved email time only, and session telemetry may overstate time in any one app.

**Legal drafting (STRONG design; law review, 2024)**
- Choi, Monahan & Schwarcz, "Lawyering in the Age of Artificial Intelligence," 109 Minn. L. Rev. 147 (2024): RCT with 60 law students each doing four tasks (complaint, contract, employee-handbook section, client memo) with or without GPT-4 after training. Quality: "slight and inconsistent" improvement; speed: large, consistent gains, reported as 12–32% depending on task; lowest-skilled gained most on quality; satisfaction higher — [SSRN 4626276](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4626276); [Univ. of Minnesota repository](https://scholarship.law.umn.edu/minnlrev/vol109/iss1/3); task-level percentages via [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/ai-helps-lawyers-complete-work-quicker-study-finds). *Caution:* students, not practitioners; 2023-era GPT-4; small n.

**Medical documentation — ambient AI scribes**
- **Multisite controlled study, JAMA 2026 (STRONG):** Rotenstein et al., "Changes in Clinician Time Expenditure and Visit Quantity With Adoption of AI-Powered Scribes: A Multisite Study," DOI 10.1001/jama.2026.2253; 5 academic systems (Mass General Brigham, Emory, UCSF, Yale New Haven, UC Davis), >1,800 scribe users vs 6,770 same-institution controls over 2+ years. Per day: documentation time −16 minutes (−10%), total EHR time −13 minutes (−3%); +0.5 visits/week; revenue effect significant but "nominal" at $167/month per adopting clinician; after-hours EHR time not significantly different. Heavy users (scribe on ≥50% of visits, only 32% of users) got about 2× the EHR-time and 3× the documentation-time reduction (secondary: 27.3 and 21.3 minutes/day) — [Mass General Brigham press release](https://news.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/ai-scribes-linked-to-modest-reductions-in-ehr-documentation-time); [2 Minute Medicine summary](https://www.2minutemedicine.com/ambient-artificial-intelligence-scribes-reduce-clinician-documentation-time-by-16-minutes-per-encounter/); [textbookofdigitalhealth summary](https://www.textbookofdigitalhealth.com/news/2026-05-18-jama-scribe-study.html). Some secondary coverage gives the cohort as 8,581 clinicians (users + controls combined). *Caution:* observational with controls, not randomized; "modest" is the authors' own word.
- **Three-arm RCT, NEJM AI 2025 (STRONG design; figures via vendor/secondary):** Lukac et al., 238 physicians across 14 specialties randomized to Nabla, Microsoft DAX, or usual care; Nabla −9.5% time-in-note vs control, DAX no significant change (p=0.66); one summary gives 41 seconds saved per note vs 18 seconds in control. Headline figures come from the vendor's release — [Nabla press release](https://nabla.com/press-release/nejm-ai-trial-reports-efficiency-gains-for-physicians-using-nablas-ambient-ai-assistant); trial registration [NCT06792890](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06792890). *Caution:* vendor-reported; effect sizes are seconds per note.
- **Stepped-wedge trial, NEJM AI 2025 (STRONG design; secondary figures):** Afshar et al.: documentation time −0.36 hours/day with no loss of note quality; share above high work-exhaustion threshold fell from 56.1% to 35.4% — [Consultant360 summary](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/ambient-ai-scribes-linked-lower-work-exhaustion-multistate-pragmatic-trial). *Caution:* secondary source; not verified against the paper.
- **Single-site before/after, JAMA Network Open 2025 (STRONG venue, weak design):** 46 Philadelphia outpatient clinicians; 20.4% less time on notes per appointment (10.3 → 8.2 minutes) but notes 20.6% longer — [Epocrates summary of JAMA Netw Open](https://epocrates.com/online/article/the-ambient-scribe-is-listening). *Caution:* small, uncontrolled.
- **Six-system QI study, JAMA Network Open Oct 2025 (STRONG venue, before/after):** 263 ambulatory clinicians; burnout prevalence 51.9% → 38.8% after 30 days; no per-note minutes reported — [textbookofdigitalhealth summary](https://www.textbookofdigitalhealth.com/news/2026-05-18-jama-scribe-study.html). *Caution:* self-reported burnout, 30-day window.
- **Kaiser / The Permanente Medical Group, NEJM Catalyst (WEAK design — internal rollout report):** 7,260 physicians, 2,576,627 encounters (Oct 2023–Dec 2024); "nearly 16,000 hours" (15,791) of documentation time saved relative to infrequent/non-users; physician survey n=102, patient survey n=118; no per-note figure; authors are TPMG staff — [Kaiser Permanente Division of Research](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/ai-assisted-notetaking-gains-steady-support-from-kaiser-permanente-physicians/); AMA coverage of the 15,000-hour figure — [AMA](https://ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/ai-scribes-save-15000-hours-and-restore-human-side-medicine). *Caution:* 15,791 hours ÷ 2.58M encounters ≈ 22 seconds per encounter; it is a large-number headline, not a large per-visit effect.

### Inferences
- For a small firm, the transferable pattern is: a bounded drafting task (quote, memo, email reply, summary) done by a non-expert gets roughly a third faster; a whole workday does not, unless the workflow itself is restructured. The 66-firm Copilot RCT is the best evidence that "give everyone a copilot" buys back roughly 1.5–2 hours a week of email and little else.
- Novice-skew is consistent across support, writing, consulting, coding and legal tasks, which supports the claim that automation substitutes for junior/admin labor more than for expert labor.
- Every setting that measured perception alongside output (METR, Atlanta Fed executives below) found perceived savings larger than measured savings; self-reported "hours saved" should be discounted.

### Gaps
- No RCT or controlled field study found that measures end-to-end admin workflows (collect records → read → key into system → produce invoice/work order) in small firms. All credible experiments are task-level or single-application.
- Accounting/tax-prep: no rigorous field study found. The only 2025 datapoint is an OpenAI/Current pilot claim of ~31% less preparer time over ~7,000 returns, with no methodology — [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/openai-codex-tax-prep-7000-return-pilot) (WEAK). A June 2025 survey "5 hours/week saved" is a forecast by respondents, not a measurement — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/tag/ai-tax-preparation) (WEAK).
- Insurance claims handling: no independent measured study found; only Cognizant pitch-deck claims (35% faster settlement, 40% adjuster productivity; no method) — [Cognizant deck](https://www.cognizant.com/assets/en_us/field-marketing/documents/CMP-005644/Cognizant-Experince-ITC-2024-NextGen-Claim-Assist-Pitch-Deck.pdf) (WEAK) — and a Bain anecdote of 10–20 minutes saved per claim at one APAC insurer — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/es-mx/insights/100-billion-dollar-opportunity-for-generative-ai-in-p-and-c-claims-handling/) (WEAK).
- Exact 2025 QJE novice figure (vs the 34% in the working paper) not confirmed from the journal text.

## Key question 2: Document processing / RPA / IDP outcomes with methodology (consultancy data, academic SME case studies, government back-office evaluations)

### Takeaway
There is almost no independently measured evidence on RPA/IDP outcomes; the best government-grade datapoint is a 2023 inspector-general audit finding that GSA's claim of 240,000 hours/year saved by bots was "inaccurate and unreliable". Vendor straight-through-processing and accuracy claims conflict by a factor of 3–6 and are measured on curated document sets.

### Cited Findings
- **GSA RPA savings audit (STRONG — US federal inspector general, 2023):** GSA's claim that its RPA program (70+ bots) saved more than 240,000 work hours annually "was inaccurate and reliable" per the GSA OIG, which found GSA "lacks evidence to support its claims that its RPA program is generating savings" — [FedWeek summary](https://www.fedweek.com/federal-managers-daily-report/claimed-savings-from-robotics-process-automation-at-gsa-not-supported-ig-says); audit report [GSA OIG A210057](https://gsaig.gov/sites/default/files/audit-reports/A210057-1%20Final%20Report.pdf); 2026 implementation follow-up [A260045](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-06/A260045-1%20Final%20Report.pdf). *Caution:* says claimed hours were unsupported, not that savings were zero; use it as a warning about unaudited "hours saved" claims.
- **Federal CIO on RPA (WEAK — policy statement):** Suzette Kent urged agencies to reinvest RPA savings and to track budget/workforce effects properly — [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/rpa-savings-federal-agencies-reinvest-suzette-kent/). A GMU study quotes an official that GAO itself uses RPA and estimates 200,000+ labor hours saved; secondhand, unaudited — [GMU](https://content.gmu.edu/news/2021-06/study-how-robotic-automation-will-make-public-sector-agencies-faster-more-efficient). *Caution:* no GAO or UK NAO evaluation of RPA savings was found.
- **Singapore General Hospital RPA (WEAK — internal QI poster, 2025):** notification-letter generation went from a 7-minute manual process to zero staff minutes; error outcomes from a survey of 32 therapists — [SGH poster](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/622/9e404de5-9479-4781-bed3-e01567005222/1039_SGH_NHIP%202025_Robotic_Process_Automation_of_Seniors.pdf). *Caution:* one task, one site, no control.
- **Vendor RPA invoice case (WEAK — Fulcrum Digital/UiPath marketing):** invoice processing 120 minutes → 5 minutes (96%), errors cut 99%, "$180k/year" at an Indian export house — [Fulcrum Digital](https://fulcrumdigital.com/case-studies/invoice-processing-automation-through-rpa/). *Caution:* proof-of-concept, self-reported.
- **IDP straight-through-processing claims (WEAK, conflicting):** vendor table: 60–85% STP for AI-driven vs 10–20% for legacy OCR; an AP survey cited elsewhere: average 32.6% of invoices run straight through; LlamaIndex: 85–92% STP for "standard document types from controlled sources"; one vendor admits 99% accuracy on clean digital invoices can fall to 85% on scanned long-tail supplier invoices — [Hypatos](https://www.hypatos.ai/knowledge-base/invoice-processing-accuracy-benchmarks-idp); [LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/ocr-automation); [imagetotable](https://imagetotable.ai/references/document-automation-exception-rates). *Caution:* definitions of STP differ (confidence thresholds, exception routing); none independently audited.
- **Academic RPA-in-SME evidence (gap):** a 2024 Theseus thesis on RPA for SME project reporting is qualitative action research with recommendations, no measured outcomes — [Theseus](https://www.theseus.fi/handle/10024/861330); a Nov 2023 EasyChair RPA case-study compilation is a preprint collection — [EasyChair](https://5wwwww.easychair.org/publications/preprint/5Vl7/open). *Caution:* neither gives usable numbers.

### Inferences
- Any RPA/IDP "hours saved" figure a small firm hears is, on current evidence, a vendor estimate; the only audited large claim was found unsupported. Honest marketing should quote the per-task minutes measured on the firm's own documents, not industry STP rates.
- The clean-vs-scanned accuracy gap (99% → 85% in a vendor's own admission) is the practical number for small firms whose inbound paperwork is photos and faxes.

### Gaps
- No Deloitte/EY/McKinsey RPA case data with stated methodology was found in this pass; their figures in circulation are client anecdotes.
- No UK NAO or US GAO evaluation of back-office RPA found.
- No peer-reviewed SME RPA case study with measured time or error outcomes found (2022–2026).

## Key question 3: Small-firm-specific evidence (accounting/bookkeeping automation, e-invoicing mandates, field-service scheduling)

### Takeaway
The one rigorous small-firm result is the Peru e-invoicing mandate (IMF/Journal of Public Economics): staggered rollout raised reported sales, purchases and value-added by over 5% in year one, concentrated in smaller firms — but it measures tax compliance, not firm productivity. Cloud-accounting and dispatch-automation evidence is vendor surveys and adoption-factor studies with no causal productivity estimate.

### Cited Findings
- **Peru VAT e-invoicing (STRONG — IMF WP 19/231, published Journal of Public Economics 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2022.104661):** staggered mandate (large firms first, SMEs later) used as difference-in-differences; reported firm sales, purchases and value-added rose by over 5% in the first year after adoption, concentrated among smaller firms and in sectors with higher non-compliance; later analysis: taxable sales +7.4%, taxable purchases +6.5% among mandated firms (working paper ITT: +6.57% sales) — [IMF WP 19/231](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2019/11/01/digitalization-to-improve-tax-compliance-evidence-from-vat-e-invoicing-in-peru-48672); [VoxDev summary](https://www.voxdev.org/topic/public-economics/digitalisation-improve-tax-compliance-evidence-vat-e-invoicing-peru); network spillovers in [IMF WP 2022/057](https://ideas.repec.org/p/imf/imfwpa/2022-057.html). *Caution:* the gain is reported (taxable) activity, i.e. compliance, not output per hour; do not quote it as "e-invoicing made firms 5% more productive".
- **Academic review of e-invoicing (STRONG venue, but notes thin evidence):** a PMC-hosted paper states there is "limited empirical evidence on how e-invoicing affects firm compliance and performance" and lists lower admin cost, better billing/payment integration and accuracy as expected, not measured, benefits — [PMC9168443](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9168443). *Caution:* confirms the gap.
- **Latin America tax-gap claims (WEAK — Sovos/Billentis vendor material):** Chile and Mexico VAT gap cut "as much as 50%", Colombia evasion cut 50%, Brazil +$58M revenue; AméricaEconomía survey: ~40% of firms experienced operational delays from e-invoicing issues — [Sovos](https://sovos.com/blog/vat/accounts-payable-latin-america-is-electronic-the-rest-of-the-world-is-paper); [Sovos/AméricaEconomía](https://sovos.com/blog/company/guest-post-from-america-economia-survey-highlights-several-pain-points-for-businesses-in-latin-america). *Caution:* tax-authority outcomes, vendor-relayed, method unknown.
- **Italy B2B e-invoicing mandate (gap):** only practitioner commentary found; no peer-reviewed firm-productivity study — [SAPinsider](https://sapinsider.org/how-sap-customers-can-meet-the-challenges-of-italys-e-invoicing-mandate/) (WEAK).
- **Cloud accounting in SMEs — adoption studies, no productivity causal estimate:** Oman ICT-sector SMEs, n=159, SEM; only compatibility significantly predicted adoption — [JSTPM 14(5), 2023](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JSTPM-08-2021-0114); Zimbabwe, 132 questionnaires, very low adoption, barriers = awareness, security, cost — [IJEFI Dec 2024](https://doaj.org/article/13b59aea566c4c1f907306c9474b256e); six small NZ accounting firms reported higher operating costs but greater organisational efficiency (2018, qualitative) — [Wintec](https://researcharchive.wintec.ac.nz/id/eprint/6214/). All STRONG venue / WEAK for productivity claims. *Caution:* these answer "who adopts", not "what it saves".
- **Xero UK industry report 2023 (WEAK — vendor survey):** 95% of practices use cloud software; practices using it for multiple tasks reported a 75% profit increase vs 39% for single-task users; micro/small practices lag — [Xero](https://blog.xero.com/2023/09/uk-accounting-bookkeeping-industry-report-2023/). *Caution:* self-reported, correlational, vendor-sponsored.
- **Field-service dispatch automation (all WEAK — vendor):** Fieldproxy HVAC client 4.5 → 6.3 jobs/tech/day in one post and 4.2 → 5.8 (38%) in another — [Fieldproxy](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/blog/hvac-company-increases-daily-jobs-by-40-with-ai-scheduling-d1-43); Upper Inc. "15–25% more jobs per technician" unsourced — [Upper](https://www.upperinc.com/?p=85807); a 2022 Service Council benchmark spread of 1.6 / 3.1 / 6.3 jobs per tech per day (low/average/high performers) describes variation between firms, not an automation effect — [StealthAgents aggregator](https://stealthagents.com/research/field-service-scheduling-workload-statistics-2026). *Caution:* no control groups, inconsistent numbers from the same vendor.
- **OECD, "Generative AI and the SME Workforce" (STRONG source, self-reported outcomes, 2025):** representative 2024 survey of 5,000+ SMEs in Austria, Canada, Germany, Ireland, Japan, Korea, UK; generative AI in use in 31% of SMEs; SMEs report improved performance and help with skill gaps/labour shortages — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-and-the-sme-workforce_2d08b99d-en). *Caution:* perceptions, not measured output.
- **OECD G7 SME AI adoption paper (STRONG source, 2025):** 11.9% of small firms (10–49 employees) use AI vs 40% of large firms; productivity premia for AI adopters "shrink significantly" once pre-existing digital capabilities, skills and complementary investments are controlled for — [OECD paper via geteasy mirror](https://assets.geteasy.ai/internal/files/oecd-report-ai-adoption-by-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises.pdf); [summary](https://www.libertify.com/interactive-library/oecd-ai-adoption-smes/). *Caution:* the "4–15% productivity premium for AI firms" circulating in summaries was not verified against the OECD text; it is selection, not treatment effect.

### Inferences
- The only small-firm effect with a credible design (Peru) is about revealing activity to the tax authority; it says nothing about hours saved in the back office. Any "e-invoicing saves X per invoice" claim rests on the vendor benchmarks in question 4.
- Small-firm AI adoption is a third of large-firm adoption (OECD), and the measured adopter premium largely reflects firms that were already digitally capable; a service that supplies the capability is addressing the actual constraint, but should not promise the premium.

### Gaps
- No causal study (DiD, RCT) of bookkeeping/cloud-accounting automation on small-firm hours, cost or error rates found for 2022–2026.
- No independent study of scheduling/dispatch automation in field service found.
- No small-firm-specific generative-AI field experiment found; all RCTs above are inside large firms or online labour markets.

## Key question 4: Cost-per-transaction benchmarks (invoice processing, data-entry errors, healthcare administrative transactions)

### Takeaway
The CAQH Index (2024 edition, 2023 data, NORC methodology, plans covering 63% of US enrolled lives) is the only STRONG per-transaction manual-vs-electronic cost and time source: e.g. a prior authorization costs providers $12.88 and 24 minutes by phone/fax versus $5.38 and 10 minutes fully electronic, and a claim status inquiry $13.80/25 minutes vs $3.64/7 minutes. Invoice-processing benchmarks ($12.88 manual-ish average vs $2.78 best-in-class) are sponsor-hosted and should be labelled WEAK; no primary source for the ubiquitous "1% data-entry error rate" exists.

### Cited Findings

**CAQH Index 2024 (STRONG — industry-wide survey, calculations by NORC; 2023 transaction data; medical plans representing 216M covered lives / 63% of US enrolled population, 3B claims, 17B transactions; dental plans 136M lives)** — [CAQH 2024 Index PDF](https://www.caqh.org/hubfs/Index/2024%20Index%20Report/CAQH_IndexReport_2024_FINAL.pdf)
- Headline: US healthcare administrative work costs $440B/yr; the Index tracks $90B of it; moving remaining manual/partial transactions to fully electronic would save $20B (22% of tracked cost); automation already avoided $222B in 2023 (+15% vs prior year).
- Per-transaction PROVIDER cost, medical, manual / partially electronic (portal) / fully electronic: eligibility & benefit verification $8.57 / $4.46 / $2.00; prior authorization $12.88 / $8.93 / $5.38; claim submission $6.33 / – / $3.05; attachments $5.54 / – / $4.51; claim status inquiry $13.80 / $5.24 / $3.64; claim payment $4.99 / – / $3.16; remittance advice $5.67 / $5.31 / $2.95. Industry (plan+provider) manual cost: PA $16.29, claim status $18.18, eligibility $12.95; industry savings per transaction moving manual→electronic: PA $10.86, claim status $14.50, eligibility $10.91, claim submission $4.20.
- Per-transaction PROVIDER time, medical, manual / partial / electronic (minutes): eligibility 16 / 8 / 4; prior authorization 24 / 16 / 10 (range 2–60 manual); claim submission 12 / – / 5; attachments 11 / – / 7; acknowledgements 12 / 9 / 4; claim status inquiry 25 / 10 / 7 (the most time-consuming task); claim payment 8 / – / 5; remittance advice 9 / 8 / 5. ### Inferences
- The CAQH numbers are the best public template for "cost per task before/after": moving a task from phone/fax to fully electronic cuts labor time by roughly 55–70% (PA 24→10 min, claim status 25→7, eligibility 16→4) and cost by 40–75%. A comparable manual→electronic ratio is the defensible range to quote for other document workflows, with the caveat that it was measured only in healthcare transactions.
- Because CAQH excludes information-gathering and follow-up, the real manual burden per task is higher than listed; conversely the "saved" minutes assume the electronic path works first time.

## Key question 5: Macro evidence on generative-AI productivity effects, especially for small firms (OECD/IMF/Fed/NBER/Census)

### Takeaway
Economy-wide measurements are small: Danish administrative data linked to 25,000 workers show users report ~3% of work hours saved and no detectable earnings or hours effects; Fed economists estimate 0.125–0.875 pp of aggregate labor-productivity gain at late-2024 usage; the Atlanta Fed's executive survey finds perceived gains exceed measured ones; Census BTOS finds 18% of firms using AI with only 2% reporting AI-related employment cuts.

### Cited Findings
- **Humlum & Vestergaard, "Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects" (STRONG — BFI WP 2025-56, Danish register data):** two adoption surveys (late 2023, 2024) of 25,000 workers at 7,000 workplaces in 11 AI-exposed occupations, linked to employer-employee data; difference-in-differences using employer policies; users report average time savings of ~3% of work hours (press coverage: 2.8%, about an hour a week); no significant effect on earnings or recorded hours in any occupation, CIs rule out effects above 1%; only 3–7% of productivity gains pass through to earnings; chatbots created new tasks for 8.4% of workers; employer-led rollout raised adoption from 47% to 83% — [BFI working paper PDF](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-3.pdf); [Marginal Revolution summary](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2025/04/large-language-models-small-labor-market-effects.html). *Caution:* Denmark, 2023–24 tools, self-reported time savings; occupations include accountants, customer support, HR, office clerks.
- **Bick, Blandin & Deming (STRONG — St. Louis Fed working paper, revised 2025):** nationally representative US survey; by late 2024, 45% of adults 18–64 use generative AI; 27% of employed used it for work in the prior week; implied aggregate labor-productivity increase of 0.125–0.875 percentage points at current usage — [St. Louis Fed WP 2024-027](https://s3.amazonaws.com/real.stlouisfed.org/wp/2024/2024-027.pdf); [FRBSF summary](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/system-research-st-louis-fed/2025/10/impact-generative-ai-work-productivity/). *Caution:* the per-user "33% faster" figure quoted by aggregators is self-reported time saved on the tasks where used, not measured output.
- **Atlanta Fed WP 2026-4, "Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce: Evidence from Corporate Executives" (STRONG source, self-reported):** survey of nearly 750 executives; more than half of firms have invested in AI, smaller firms only beginning; labor-productivity gains positive, largest in high-skill services and finance, expected to strengthen in 2026; perceived gains larger than measured gains ("likely a delay in revenue realization"); little near-term aggregate job loss, larger firms expect cuts while smaller firms expect modest employment gains — [Atlanta Fed](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/working-papers/2026/03/25/04-artificial-intelligence-productivity-and-the-workforce-evidence-from-corporate-executives). A secondary blog reports "90% of executives say AI hasn't boosted productivity" and ~1.8% average gain in 2025; not verified against the paper — [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/ai-productivity-paradox-executives-atlanta-fed-fortune-2026) (WEAK). Companion WP 2026-3 surveyed ~6,000 CFOs/CEOs in US, UK, Germany, Australia and finds ~70% of firms actively use AI, skewed to younger, more productive firms — [Atlanta Fed WP 2026-3](https://atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/working-papers/2026/03/24/03-firm-data-on-ai). *Caution:* executive self-report; percentages not on the abstract page.
- **Census BTOS AI supplement (STRONG — official statistics; CES-WP-26-25 / NBER w35141, data Nov 2025–Jan 2026):** 18% of firms use AI in at least one business function (32% employment-weighted), expected 22% within six months; 66% of users use AI solely to augment tasks; AI-related employment decreases in only 2% of firms; breadth of AI integration is positively correlated with commercial performance (correlation, not causal) — [Census WP](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/CES-WP-26-25.html); [NBER w35141](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35141). Earlier BTOS: nearly all adopters report no employment change and causal attribution is "difficult" — [Jarmin slides](https://geospatialworld.net/gwf/2026/presentations/plenary/plenary6/the-double-whammy-of-ai-for-official-statistics_ron-s-jarmin.pdf). Census story May 2026: AI use 17–20% Dec 2025–May 2026, firms with 20+ employees the biggest users — [Census](https://census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html). *Caution:* the BTOS AI question changed in Nov 2025, so the series is not continuous with 2023–24 readings.
- **OECD SME evidence:** see Key question 3 (11.9% small-firm adoption; adopter premium shrinks with controls; 31% of SMEs using generative AI, self-reported performance gains).

### Inferences
- Macro and register-data studies converge on low single-digit percent time savings at the worker level and sub-1pp aggregate productivity effects as of 2024–25, which is consistent with the Copilot RCT (email only) and inconsistent with vendor "30–50% of admin time" claims applied firm-wide.
- The gap between task-level RCT gains (10–40%) and firm-level measurements (~3%) is the adoption/redesign gap: tools used in a third of weeks, on a subset of tasks, without workflow change. A service that does the redesign and runs the workflow is selling the missing piece — but the evidence base for that service model itself is not yet published.

### Gaps
- No IMF 2025–26 paper specifically on generative AI and SME productivity found.
- NBER w34851 (Cruces et al., 2026, "Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps?") exists but no results were retrieved — [NBER w34851](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34851).
- Atlanta Fed productivity percentages (overall and by sector) are in the PDF, not the abstract; not extracted.
