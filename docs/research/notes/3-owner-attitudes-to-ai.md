# US small-business owners' attitudes to AI, 2023–2026: interest vs. adoption, the "where do I start" gap, barriers, trust, and the help they want

Compiled 2026-10-28. Each finding gives the figure, who measured it, the wave/date, the sample, the URL, a one-line stretch-caution, and a STRONG/WEAK grade (STRONG = government, academic, or large-sample with published methodology; WEAK = vendor/sponsor survey, small sample, or marketing). Where I could only reach a figure through secondary coverage, that is flagged. Nothing here is invented; gaps are listed per section.

A note on why the headline numbers disagree by 4x: "AI use" ranges from 18% (Census BTOS, "in any business function", firm-weighted, all US firms including non-employers) to 46% (Fed SBCS, employer firms, "business or its employees use AI for work", convenience sample) to 66–77% (Chamber, Bank of America, Intuit, Goldman: sponsor surveys with broad "any AI-enabled tool" definitions and samples skewed to engaged/online owners). The gradient is the finding: the broader the definition and the more self-selected the sample, the higher the number. The Census working paper itself attributes a discrete jump in late 2025 partly to question wording.

## Key question 1: Adoption — what share of US small firms actually use AI?

### Takeaway
On the most rigorous measure (Census BTOS, ~1.2M-firm sample frame, biweekly), 17–20% of all US firms used AI in any business function in Dec 2025–May 2026, under 20% for firms with 4 or fewer employees, versus 37% at 250+ employees; and before the November 2025 wording change the "AI in producing goods or services" rate was only 3.9% (Nov 2023) rising to ~10% (Sept 2025). Among employer firms in the Fed's 2025 SBCS, 46% use AI but only 7% of users call it "fully integrated." Sponsor surveys (Chamber 66%, Goldman 68–76%, BofA 77%, Intuit 77%) are 3–4x higher because of definition and sample.

### Cited Findings

**US Census Bureau, Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) — STRONG**
- Dec 14, 2025–May 3, 2026: overall current AI use "hovered between 17% and 20%"; expected use in the next six months 20–23%; national rate 19.8% as of May 3, 2026. By size: 250+ employees 37%; 100–249 employees 32% (period ending May 3, 2026); firms with at least 20 employees increased use between Dec 2025 and May 2026 while firms under 20 "didn't change significantly"; "less than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees reported using AI." By sector (May 3, 2026): Information 39.7% (≈42% expected), Finance & Insurance 33.9% (≈39% expected), Retail ≈14% (≈17% expected). Question wording was revised in November 2025 from AI "in producing goods or services" to AI "in any business function"; the second AI supplement began Nov 17, 2025 and covers 15 business functions plus reasons for non-use. Survey is biweekly and nationally representative; the page does not give the response count. — [Census story, "Large Firms With at Least 20 Employees Biggest AI Users", May 26, 2026](https://census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html). Caution: firm-weighted (every firm counts once, so dominated by micro-firms); pre/post-Nov-2025 series are not comparable.
- Nov 2023 baseline under the old wording: "an estimated 3.9% of businesses used AI to produce goods or services between Oct. 23 and Nov. 5, 2023." — [Census story, Nov 2023](https://census.gov/library/stories/2023/11/businesses-use-ai.html) (figure confirmed via search snippet of the Census page; the page itself was not fetched). Caution: narrow "in production" definition; not comparable to 2026 numbers.
- Pre-revision series: biweekly AI-use estimates rose from 3.7% to 5.4% with an expected 6.6% by early fall 2024. — [Census CES-WP-24-16, "Tracking Firm Use of AI in Real Time"](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2024/adrm/CES-WP-24-16.html). Caution: same narrow definition.
- Economic Innovation Group's reading of the same BTOS series: "the percent of firms using AI, by this definition, has crept up from 4.6 percent at the beginning of 2024 to 10 percent in September 2025." — [EIG analysis](https://eig.org/?p=24816). Caution: secondary analysis of Census data; EIG notes the "in production" measure is restrictive.

**US Census Bureau working paper CES-WP-26-25 (Bonney, Breaux, Dinlersoz, Foster, Haltiwanger, Pande), April 2026 — STRONG**
- 2026 AI supplement, reference period Nov 17, 2025–Jan 2026 (six biweekly panels drawn from the ~1.2 million-business BTOS sample): 18% of firms used AI in a business function (32% employment-weighted); expected 22% within six months. Pre-revision firm-weighted use rose from 3.5% to 10%, then "reaching 18% in the post-revision phase." The jump is attributed to "(i) revised core question wording, (ii) continued adoption during the lapse in federal funding ... and (iii) the introduction of the second AI supplement, which provided additional context and likely increased the propensity to report use." — [EconPapers entry](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/cenwpaper/26-25.htm); [PDF](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf). Caution: the authors themselves say part of the late-2025 rise is measurement, not adoption.
- By size class (post-revision supplement period): firms under 20 employees "around 18%, up from 10%"; 250+ employees 31% (from 13%). Expectation gap widens with size: 1–4 employees expect 21% vs 18% current; 100–249 expect 34% vs 25% current; 250+ expect 40% vs 31%. Pre-revision, 1–4 employee firms drifted from ~4% to ~10% while 250+ went from ~5% to 13%. — same PDF, section 3.3. Caution: size-class figures in the paper (31% for 250+) differ slightly from the May 2026 Census story (37%) because of different reference periods.
- Depth: 57% of adopting firms use AI in three or fewer business functions; 65% limit AI to three or fewer worker tasks; 66% of users rely on AI only to augment tasks; AI-related employment decreases in only 2% of firms. Top functions among adopters: Sales & Marketing 52%, Strategy/Business Development 45%, IT 41%. Latent-class analysis: non-users 72.3% of firms; "Marketing Specialists" (avg 18 employees) and "Minimalist Adopters" dominate among users; comprehensive adoption "remains a rare event." Integration index: 67.9% of firms score 0, 25.1% score in (0, 0.25]. — same PDF. Caution: firm-weighted; these are share-of-firms, not share-of-economy.
- Stickiness: 71.5% of current non-users intend to remain non-adopters in the next six months, 19.1% have no clear plans, only 9.4% plan to switch (17.7% employment-weighted). — same PDF, Table 1. Caution: intentions, not realized behavior.

**Federal Reserve Banks, Small Business Credit Survey, 2026 Report on Employer Firms (2025 SBCS) — STRONG (large sample, published methodology) but a convenience sample**
- Fielded Sept 3–Nov 14, 2025; 6,525 responses from "a nationwide convenience sample of small employer firms with 1–499 full- or part-time employees"; "The SBCS is not a random sample; results should be analyzed with awareness of potential biases." AI definition shown to respondents: "Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to technology designed to perform and learn tasks that typically require human intelligence ... AI tools can include stand-alone AI applications or features of software you already use." — [2026 Report on Employer Firms PDF](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms/2026-report-on-employer-firms.pdf). Caution: employer firms only; respondents recruited through partner organizations, so likely more engaged than average.
- Use (N=5,248): "Business currently uses AI" 46%; "Not using but plan to use in next 12 months" 15%; "Not using and have no plans to use" 33%; Unsure 6%. — same PDF, p.26.
- Depth among users (N=2,199): "Experimenting with AI (e.g., testing tools or running pilots)" 49%; "Partially integrated (e.g., AI is used in some business processes)" 44%; "Fully integrated (e.g., AI is embedded in business functions)" 7%. Importance to core goods/services (N=2,127): very important 21%, somewhat 42%, not important 37%. — same PDF, p.26.
- Tasks among users (N=2,225): writing or marketing 83%; individual productivity 61%; planning or analysis 51%; administrative business functions 38%; customer service 29%; process automation 21%; coding 19%. — same PDF, p.27.
- Effects: 71% of users said AI increased productivity, 39% improved quality, 31% higher sales; "the vast majority" saw no change in labor costs; among the N=373 reporting labor-cost changes, 10% increased and 11% decreased headcount because of AI. — same PDF, pp.3, 27.
- Regional: Philadelphia Fed Third District, "just over one-third" of firms use AI vs 46% nationally. — [Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/community-development/credit-and-capital/small-business-credit-survey-2026-insights-into-the-third-district-region). Caution: regional subsample.

**NFIB Research Center, Small Business and Technology Survey, June 2025 — STRONG-ish (member sample, published tables; small N)**
- Fielded by email Mar 6–31, 2025 to a random sample of 20,000 NFIB members; 521 completed. Table "Do you currently use any AI technologies for any business activities (ChatGPT, Copilot, Grammarly, Canva, Lumen5, etc.)?" Yes: non-employers 18%, 1–9 employees 21%, 10–24 employees 24%, 25–49 employees 28%, 50+ employees 48%, all employers 24% (N=514). — [NFIB PDF](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-NFIB-Technology-Survey.pdf); [NFIB release](https://www.nfib.com/?p=38888). Caution: NFIB members skew older, more established, Main Street; cells under N=30 suppressed; the question's examples (Grammarly, Canva) make this a generous definition and it still comes out at 24%.

### Inferences
- The defensible public claim is "roughly one in five US small firms uses AI in any business function (Census, 2026); fewer than one in five with four or fewer employees." Anything above 45% should be attributed to its sponsor and described as "say they use any AI-enabled tool."
- Even in the friendliest samples, deep use is rare: 7% "fully integrated" (Fed), 14% "fully integrating into core operations" (Goldman graduates), ~10% paying for dedicated AI tools (Intuit payments data), 57% of Census adopters in three or fewer functions. The modal "AI user" is a marketing/writing dabbler.
- Non-use is sticky: 71.5% of Census non-users plan to stay non-users; 75% of NFIB non-users have no plan to start within 12 months (81% among 1–9 employee firms).

## Key question 2: The gap — interest vs. implementation; "don't know where to start"; lack of time, skills, task clarity, accuracy, security, ROI

### Takeaway
The best government evidence says the number one stated reason for not adopting is "AI is not applicable to my business" (65% of non-planning firms in Census; 56% in the Fed SBCS), with "lack of knowledge of AI capabilities" a distant second (22% Census). Among firms that do use or plan to use AI, the dominant friction is not cost but "finding/adapting tools to meet business needs" (54% of planners, Fed) and accuracy (46% of users, Fed); "time to implement or train employees" is 37% for planners. The literal "don't know where to start" wording appears in sponsor surveys at 40% (Goldman graduates not using AI, undated infographic) and 12% (Bluehost, top-concern framing).

### Cited Findings

**Census BTOS 2026 AI supplement (CES-WP-26-25), Question 35, firms not planning near-term AI use — STRONG**
- "the most common barrier is that AI is not applicable to the business (65% of firms, firm-weighted)"; "Lack of knowledge of the capabilities of AI comes at a distant second (22%), followed by concerns about privacy/security (20%). The least common barriers are using vendors or consulting services to implement AI and laws and regulations that prevent or restrict use of AI." — [PDF](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf), section 6/Figure 19. Caution: asked only of firms with no near-term plans; the full Figure 19 option list with every percentage is in a figure I could not read as text, so only the three narrated values are quoted.
- The authors call the 65% a "perceptual lag ... a potential disconnect between latent technological potential and realized managerial awareness." — same PDF. Caution: authors' interpretation.

**Fed SBCS 2026 Report on Employer Firms — STRONG (convenience sample)**
- "REASONS BUSINESSES DO NOT PLAN TO USE AI (% of employer firms that do not plan to use AI, N=1,855)": "AI is not applicable to my business" 56%; "Prefer not to use" 30%; "Finding the right tool(s) to meet business needs" 19%; "Data security and privacy concerns" 18%; "Accuracy (e.g., bias or misinformation)" 17%; "Ethical/social concerns" 16%; "Cost" 13%; "Too difficult or confusing" 13%. — [PDF](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms/2026-report-on-employer-firms.pdf), p.27. ### Inferences
- The gap has two populations with different stories. (a) The "not applicable" majority of non-planners (56–65%) is a framing problem: they do not see their paperwork as AI work. (b) The interested-but-stuck minority (Fed planners, 15% of employer firms) names tool-finding (54%) and time to implement/train (37%) over cost (32%): they want someone to pick and install, not a cheaper subscription.
- "Don't know where to start" as literal wording is a sponsor-survey phrase (Goldman 40% of non-users, Bluehost 12% as top concern). In government data the same idea shows up as "lack of knowledge of AI capabilities" (22% Census; 28% Intuit US) and "finding the right tools" (19% of Fed non-planners; 54% of planners). Quote those wordings when precision matters.
- Trust/accuracy is the top in-use problem (Fed users 46%; ABA lawyers 75%), while cost is consistently mid-pack (Fed non-planners 13%, Bluehost 5%, Intuit "not the biggest hurdle").

### Gaps
- No government survey asks "don't know where to start" verbatim; the Census Figure 19 full option list could not be extracted from the PDF figure.
- No source found that pairs "interested/curious" with "implemented" in the same question for a representative US small-firm sample; the closest are NFIB (42% say AI is important now vs 24% using) and Fed (46% using, 15% planning, 33% no plans).
- Comerica per-item concern percentages not found (primary press release 404).

## Key question 3: Who helps them — do owners want a person to show them (consultants, peers, accountants, IT providers) rather than self-serve tools?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin and mostly sponsor-driven, but it points one way: 73% of Goldman-surveyed owners say they would benefit from training and implementation resources and 85% back SBA/SBDC-delivered AI help; NFIB finds vendors (28%) are the main source of new-tech ideas, followed by other owners (13%) and employees (13%); the Fed finds planners' top problem is finding/adapting tools (54%), and Census finds "using vendors or consulting services" is among the least-cited barriers, i.e., hiring help is not what stops them.

### Inferences
- There is no representative US survey that asks owners "would you rather have a person set this up or do it yourself?" That supports a "someone shows me, on my actual paperwork" offer better than a self-serve tool, but say "evidence suggests," not "owners say."
- "Pre-packaged" and "specific to my business" (Bluehost 19%/27%) and "finding/adapting tools to meet business needs" (Fed 43–54%) are the same complaint: generic tools do not map to their workflow.

### Gaps
- No SCORE, SBA or SBDC survey data on where owners get AI advice was found.
- No US survey found with a "consultant," "IT provider" or "accountant" answer option on an AI-help question.
- Chamber reports do not publish "where owners learn about technology."

## Key question 4: Expectations — what owners expect AI to do, and any willingness-to-pay or budget figures

### Takeaway
Owners expect time and admin relief first: Fed users report productivity gains (71%) far more than sales gains (31%); the dominant use everywhere is writing/marketing (83% Fed; 52% Census adopters; 29% communications + 27% marketing NFIB); professionals expect ~5 hours/week saved (Thomson Reuters). No credible US small-business willingness-to-pay or AI budget figure was found; the only hard spend signals are Intuit's payment data (~1 in 10 US SMBs pay for AI; 86% of 2024 payers still paying in 2025) and BofA Institute's "tech services incl. AI" payments up ~8% YoY.

### Gaps
- No US small-business willingness-to-pay, price-sensitivity, or monthly AI budget figure from a credible source (searched Chamber, NFIB, Fed, Intuit, Goldman, Bluehost, CloudZero).
- No survey asking whether owners see AI as a substitute for an unfilled hire.
