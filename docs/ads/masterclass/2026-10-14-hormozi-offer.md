# Offer construction: SKOX offer review (2026-10-14)
Framework: "$100M Offers" (Alex Hormozi, 2021) and "$100M Leads" (Alex Hormozi, 2023). Elements used: market selection ("starving crowd"), the Value Equation, the Grand Slam Offer build (dream outcome, problems, solutions, delivery vehicles, trim and stack), enhancers (scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, M-A-G-I-C naming), lead magnets, and the Core Four (context only).

Focus: the eight `/for/[trade]` landing pages and the live Google Search Trades campaign.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The trade pages make a fair offer but a weak *Grand Slam* offer. They pick the right pains in the owner's own words (`content/trades.ts:83-85`, `:225-227`) and the right risk reducers (fixed price and date in writing, you own the code, you keep the plan: `content/landing.ts:131-135`, `:268-272`). What's being sold, though, is a *build process* ("{keyword}. Built at speed.", `app/for/[trade]/page.tsx:88-90`), not an *outcome*. Under the Value Equation, the weakest driver is **Perceived Likelihood**: there's no proof (`components/Proof.tsx:10`), the demo is an honest concept (`app/for/[trade]/page.tsx:142`), and there's no guarantee. Next weakest are **Effort & Sacrifice** and **Time Delay** for the *first step*: the only conversion is a full scope form at the bottom of the page, followed by a reply, a call and then a plan (`docs/ads/reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:92`, `:114`). The data is too thin to show a pattern (10 clicks, 0 leads, `docs/ads/reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:79-81`). Still, the Clarity recordings (0 taps in 7 paid sessions, `:113`) fit an offer that asks for too much, too early, from someone with no reason yet to believe. The cheapest fixes are (1) putting the existing 14-day dashboard claim where a Time Delay driver should be on the three dashboard trades, and (2) a **free-sample lead magnet**: their own numbers or price list loaded into the example they just played with. At about 10 clicks a week, a high-touch magnet costs the two founders very little.

## What not to change, and why

- **The risk reducers already on the page.** These are the fixed price and date in writing before you pay, you own the code, and you keep the plan either way (`content/landing.ts:191-193`, `:271`). In this framework they're the strongest Perceived Likelihood levers SKOX can honestly pull without proof. Stack them; don't trim them.
- **The honest "concept with invented sample data" label** (`app/for/[trade]/page.tsx:142`). Removing it would trade a small conversion gain for invented proof (README rule 4).
- **No price on the page** (`docs/ads/offer.md:53`). A "from $X" price would filter leads (`docs/ads/niche-strategy.md:39`), but it's Josh's call. This framework doesn't require a published price to build a Grand Slam offer.
- **Scarcity stays off unless it's real** (`content/site.ts:375`). The only honest version is a true weekly cap on free samples or build slots, stated with its reason.
- **One page per trade with the keyword in H1 line 1.** That's the "announce who it's for" part of naming, and it's what keeps ad-to-page match.
- **Don't raise bids to chase bare "{trade} software"** to fix the offer. Those searchers want a subscription with a free trial (`docs/ads/research/competitors-2026-10-14.md:23`). Change the offer they meet, not the auction.
