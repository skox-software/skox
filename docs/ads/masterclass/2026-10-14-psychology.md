# Buyer psychology: SKOX offer review (2026-10-14)
Framework: Robert Cialdini, "Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion" (1984; new and expanded edition 2021) and "Pre-Suasion" (2016); Rory Sutherland, "Alchemy: The Surprising Power of Ideas That Don't Make Sense" (2019). Elements used: Cialdini's seven principles (reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity) and pre-suasion; Sutherland's anxiety reduction, costly signals, framing and context, and psycho-logic.

Scope: the `/for/[trade]` pages and the Google Search Trades offer that leads to them. All behavioral evidence comes from 7 Clarity recordings and 10 paid clicks (`docs/ads/reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:76-117`). That's enough to form hypotheses but not to prove anything (README rule 6).

## Verdict in one paragraph

The trade page already handles the rational side well. Price, date and ownership are spelled out (`content/trades.ts:163`, `app/for/[trade]/page.tsx:105-110`), and the form is built to lower friction (`components/ConversionForm.tsx:258-260`, `content/landing.ts:274`, `:288-289`). What it doesn't do is lower anxiety at the moment a visitor has to decide. The visitors behaved like interested people who were never shown a step small or safe enough to take. One read for about 5 active minutes and came back the next morning. One read the hero and pains for a minute. None of them tapped anything (`reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:105-116`). Three things that reduce anxiety exist only at the very bottom of the page, in the last ~20% that nobody reached (`:114`):
- what you walk away with from the free call (`landing.ts:268-271`, rendered only at `page.tsx:223-230`)
- who you'll be talking to (founder names and photos exist at `landing.ts:223-235` but never render on `/for/`; only a one-line mention does, at `page.tsx:109`)
- a first step that takes one tap

The searcher also arrives with a subscription frame of mind ("landscaping software" usually means a free trial at a monthly price, per `research/landing-offers-2026-10.md:93,100`). The page then asks for a custom-project conversation with no price on it. In Sutherland's terms the problem is uncertainty, not value. The fix is to bring the anxiety reducers that already exist up the page and add a smaller first step. None of that needs a new claim.

## What not to change, and why
- **The "concept with invented sample data" label (`page.tsx:142`).** It works as a costly honesty signal. Removing it would trade trust for a moment of looking impressive.
- **Project-first form order and "Your details come after" (`ConversionForm.tsx:258-260`, `landing.ts:274`).** This is the right commitment ladder.
- **The unfinished-enquiry notice in `formReassure` (`landing.ts:288-289`).** The comment there says it must stay, and being transparent about it lowers anxiety more than hiding it would.
- **No scarcity or urgency** (`claims.md:68`). With anxious, research-mode visitors, false urgency usually raises suspicion rather than action, and here it's forbidden anyway.
- **The CTA wording "Get my price & timeline" (`landing.ts:107`).** It names what the buyer wants most. The fix is to show the steps that come after it (change 1), not to rename it.
- **The phone line in the hero and sticky bar (`page.tsx:101-103`, `TradeStickyBar.tsx:45-47`).** A visible number is a cheap costly signal ("real people answer") even if almost nobody calls.
- **Keep the homepage client list and pipeline count off `/for/`** (`claims.md:71`).
