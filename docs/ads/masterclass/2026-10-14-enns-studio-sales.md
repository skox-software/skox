# Studio sales and pricing: SKOX offer review (2026-10-14)
Framework: "The Win Without Pitching Manifesto" (Blair Enns, 2010) and "Pricing Creativity: A Guide to Profit Beyond the Billable Hour" (Blair Enns, 2018). Elements used: Specialization, Expert vs vendor (who leads), Diagnose before you prescribe, Don't give away the solution for free, Value-based pricing (price the outcome, not the hours), Know the buyer and what the outcome is worth, Option pricing (three options).

This review applies the published themes of those books. It doesn't speak for the author. Where the books don't answer a question (for example, Google bid tactics), I say so.

Focus: the eight `/for/[trade]` pages and the Google Search offer that points at them.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The trade pages are the most expert-sounding thing SKOX has published. They open with the owner's own problem ("Sound familiar?", `app/for/[trade]/page.tsx:113-124`) and tell the buyer where to start ("Start with the one that hurts most.", `page.tsx:164`). The offer around them still reads like a vendor's. Every button asks for a quote ("Get my price & timeline", `content/landing.ts:107`). The form asks the buyer to say what to build ("What would you like to build?", `landing.ts:279`). The free call promises the full prescription, a written scope, fixed price and delivery date, with "the plan is yours, whether you hire us or not" (`landing.ts:268-272`). Under the Diagnose before you prescribe theme, SKOX gives away the prescription for free and invites the buyer to shop it around. It doesn't charge for the diagnosis, it doesn't sort serious buyers from browsers before the free work, and it offers one price where it could offer options. That's the advice the roofing page gives roofers ("A single number invites a comparison. Good, better and best invites a decision.", `content/trades.ts:199`), and SKOX doesn't follow it yet. On Specialization, eight trades and two product types share $9.80 a day (`docs/ads/reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:46`). That's specialization in nouns only: one generic offer with the trade name swapped in. The "{trade} software" keywords also put SKOX in front of people shopping for subscriptions (`reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:91`), which is the setting the framework warns about most, where a generalist gets compared on price. Keep the free call, because these are small trades and there are 0 leads so far. Change what the call produces, add three options, ask qualifying questions before the free work, and narrow paid spend and outreach to one buyer.

What's free: the fit check, the diagnosis, and a one-page priced plan with three options. What's paid: the detailed specification, and full scoping for open-ended products. That keeps the low barrier small trades need while keeping the part that's valuable to a price shopper (the detailed plan) behind the deposit.

## What not to change, and why

- **The free 30-minute call itself** (`landing.ts:109`, C04). These buyers are small trades. With 0 leads and about 10 paid clicks (`reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:79-81`), a paid first step would remove the only entry point. The framework's point is to stop giving away the *prescription*, not to charge for the first conversation.
- **No public price** (`offer.md:53`). Value-based pricing needs the diagnosis first. Publishing a number would set a cost-based anchor before the buyer has said what the problem is worth. A "from $X" (`landing-offers-2026-10.md:146`) runs against this lens. It might filter leads, but option pricing and the qualifying fields (changes 2 and 4) do the same job without fixing a price.
- **The pains sections and "Start with the one that hurts most."** (`page.tsx:113-124`, `:164`). This is the expert voice. Keep it and extend it.
- **The concept label** ("A concept with invented sample data, not a client's system", `page.tsx:142`). An honest example supports expertise. Fake proof would undermine it.
- **The scorecard as the lighter step** (`page.tsx:194-203`). It's free help that shows SKOX's thinking without giving away the build, and it's where the out-of-fit buyers in step 1 go.
- **The pinned audience qualifiers** ("For Roofing Contractors" etc., `reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:71`). They're a small act of specialization inside the ad.
