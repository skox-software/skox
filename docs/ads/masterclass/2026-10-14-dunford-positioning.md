# Positioning — SKOX offer review (2026-10-14)
Framework: "Obviously Awesome" (April Dunford, 2019) and "Sales Pitch" (April Dunford, 2023). Elements used: Competitive alternatives, Unique attributes, Value (and proof), Best-fit customer characteristics, Market category (head-to-head / subsegment / new category), and the Sales Pitch sequence (Insight → Perfect solution → Differentiated value → Proof → Ask).

Scope: the eight trade landing pages (`content/trades.ts`, rendered by `app/for/[trade]/page.tsx`) and the live Google Search – Trades campaign. Data caveat: 10 paid clicks, 0 leads and 7 Clarity sessions (`docs/ads/reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:76-117`). Nothing below treats that data as a proven pattern. It is used only to show what the searcher saw.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The trade pages position SKOX against the wrong alternative. The homepage frames the choice as "big dev shop vs freelancer vs SKOX" (`content/landing.ts:142-176`). That's the right frame for someone searching "custom software", but the `/for/` pages don't render it (`docs/ads/campaigns/google-search-v2.md:169`), and the trade searcher isn't comparing studios anyway. Their competitive alternative is an off-the-shelf subscription app plus spreadsheets, a whiteboard and the phone (`docs/ads/research/competitors-2026-10-14.md:12-23`, and SKOX's own pain copy at `content/trades.ts:83`, `:169`). On the first screen, the only category signal is the word "Custom" in the H1 (`content/trades.ts:158`). The page never says what SKOX is instead of a subscription, how it relates to the job software the owner already pays for, or why owning it matters. The trust strip that says "You own the code" stacks below the hero on phones (`app/for/[trade]/trades.css:375-378`), and 3 of the 5 landscaping visits were on iPhone (`reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:105-109`). SKOX has an evidenced and genuinely unoccupied position. In ads, the subscription vendors are trade-specific but off the shelf, and the studios are custom but generic (`competitors-2026-10-14.md:55`). The recommended category is a **big fish in a subsegment**: custom software for multi-crew home-service companies, built alongside the job software they already use and owned outright. It should not go head-to-head as "landscaping software" against subscription vendors, and it should not try to create a new category, which two founders on $300/month can't afford to educate.

## What not to change, and why
- **The pains → builds mapping** (`trades.ts:82-91`, `:169-177`, `:196-205`). This is where value is stated in the customer's terms (Value element). Keep it word for word and add the alternatives block around it.
- **The concept label on every demo** (`page.tsx:142`). Under the Proof element, an honest "concept" beats implied proof, and claims rules forbid the alternative (`claims.md:60`).
- **No price on the page** (`claims.md:63`). A subscription-style price would pull SKOX back into the head-to-head category it should leave.
- **Keep one trade per page.** Under Best-fit, the trade is the segment label and the positioning statement stays the same across all eight. Don't write eight different positionings.
- **Don't lead with "own it, don't rent it" as *replace your subscription*.** The review's recommendation B (`reviews/2026-10-11-google.md:96`) and `competitors-2026-10-14.md:66` are directionally right on ownership. But "replace your field-service software" is a far bigger build than the evidenced builds (`trades.ts:87-290`), and two founders can't credibly promise it. Ownership should attach to *the piece SKOX builds*, as in changes #2–#3.
- **Don't adopt "Dream it, build it"** language (`competitors-2026-10-14.md:51`, `:69`). That category belongs to DIY builders, and it's the wrong frame of reference for an owner with a budget.
- **Don't add a homepage-style "Why SKOX vs dev shop / freelancer" table to `/for/` pages.** It names the wrong alternatives for this searcher.

## Open questions for Josh (only facts the repo can't answer)
1. **Alongside or replacement?** Will SKOX build only the piece their job software lacks, or also offer to replace it outright? The category line in #2 and the insight in #3 assume "alongside".
2. **Best-fit size:** what is the minimum build price you'd accept from a trade lead, and roughly how many crews or locations must a company have to afford it? (`economics.md:20-22` is blank.) Without this, the landscaping page can't carry a size qualifier like HVAC's "more than one crew".
3. **Approve or reject the new wording:** "Not another subscription app", the insight line, and the left-column alternative descriptions in #3.
4. **Proof:** which shipped dashboard or tool, if any, can be shown, and has that client agreed in writing? (`niche-strategy.md:87`)
5. **Which trade do you or Reiss know best firsthand?** The framework puts the most effort into the segment that cares most. The repo shows no trade relationship among the eight (Ireland Surveying is surveying, `niche-strategy.md:18`).
